  • Dollar — eased today after yesterday's June high: The Dollar Index sits at ~101.30, down 0.12% d/d, but was at 101.60 yesterday — a June high — heading into today's FOMC decision. 🟡 Choppy — today's move is event-driven (cooling Mideast tensions + a -6% WTI crude plunge), not a clean trend change.
  • Strait of Hormuz — still critical, structurally unresolved: Non-Iranian ship transits remain near 25 vessels vs. a 108 pre-crisis baseline (~90% YoY decline), and Houthi forces continue threatening a Saudi-port "maritime embargo" that could extend into the Red Sea. Today's news specifically noted a third straight day without a direct US-Iran strike. 🟢 long-term dollar-supportive / 🟡 today's specific lull eased the safe-haven bid — a sudden re-escalation or a real ceasefire could each move this quickly.

  • Fed — the dominant catalyst, decision due this afternoon: FOMC decision due today, Jul 29 at 2:00pm ET (press conference 2:30pm ET); markets price ~68.5% odds of a hold at 3.50–3.75%, ~31.5% odds of a hike. A hawkish hold/statement would be favorable for you; any dovish pivot on rate-cut timing would work against further gains. 🟡 The key event risk in the next few hours — outcome not yet known as of this report.

APEX Take — Conversion Strategy

€ per $1 eased to €0.8780 today, down about 0.3% from yesterday's €0.8807 six-month high, as the dollar softened on cooling Mideast tensions and a sharp oil price drop that lowered inflation expectations. You're still getting meaningfully more euro per dollar than six months ago (+3.8%), but today's pullback is a reminder the trend isn't one-directional — and the Fed's decision this afternoon (2:00pm ET) is the next real swing factor.

Recommendation: Hold off on converting a large lump sum until today's 2:00pm ET FOMC announcement lands. If the Fed holds with hawkish language, convert 30–40% promptly and stage the rest around the Aug 12 CPI print. If the Fed signals a dovish pivot toward rate cuts, wait — the rate is more likely to soften further, and it may be worth holding for the next Hormuz-driven dollar spike before converting more than a small tranche. Keep watching the Strait of Hormuz situation — it remains the single biggest swing factor over the next two weeks. As always, a forward contract or rate-alert/limit order via your bank's FX desk is a better tool than manual timing for the bulk of the balance.
