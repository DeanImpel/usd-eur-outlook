1. Bottom Line

🔴 Every dollar you convert today buys ~€0.855 — the weakest level in the entire six-month window, down 0.15% on the day and 1.36% on the week after the dollar's worst session since May. This is not a good window to convert, but it is also not a window to wait out passively: the slide is being driven by a US Treasury liquidity decision rather than a data surprise, which means there is no scheduled event before Jackson Hole (Aug 27–29) or July PCE (Aug 28) that plausibly reverses it — so the honest read is convert only what you need now and put the rest on a dated deadline, not on hope.
  • Six months ago (March) $1 bought roughly €0.8642 on average. Today $1 gets you €0.8552 — a 1.04% half-year loss, and the first time this window has printed below the April trough of €0.8557. The €0.8807 peak of Jul 28 is now 3.0% above you.
  • Wednesday was the break, not a drift. EUR/USD jumped 0.86% in a single session to 1.1676, a three-month high, and has extended to 1.1693 today. In € per $1 terms that is €0.8623 → €0.8552 in two sessions. The prior guidance to convert immediately was right for four straight days; anyone still holding is now converting into the worst print of the half-year.
  • The cause was fiscal plumbing, not the Fed. The US Treasury announced it will at least double its long-dated buyback operations to $4bn+ per operation from September 9, pulling the 30-year yield back from a 19-year high and the 10-year to 4.65%. Lower long-end yields and more dollar liquidity out of the TGA = a weaker dollar. Fewer euro per dollar, and this is a policy stance rather than a data point, so it does not simply mean-revert.
  • The July FOMC minutes leaned hawkish and the market ignored them. That is the tell. When a hawkish surprise cannot lift the dollar, positioning and flow — not rate expectations — are running the tape. September is still priced around a hold at the Sept 15–16 FOMC. Fewer euro per dollar.
  • The euro leg remains expensive and the Hormuz bid now cuts against you. Roughly 84% odds are priced for a 25bp ECB hike on Sept 10, and soaring European gas prices from Middle East supply shortages are reinforcing that case rather than undermining it. The energy shock that was helping you in July is now feeding euro-area inflation and therefore ECB hawkishness. Fewer euro per dollar on both counts.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — 2½-month low, worst session since May: DXY sits near 98.83, down 0.76–0.85% on Wednesday and now at its lowest since early June. The index has lost roughly 2.7% from the late-July high of 101.60 and the break below 100 has been decisively confirmed. 🔴 The single largest driver of your rate, and it is trending against you.
  • US Treasury — the new swing factor: Treasury will at least double long-dated buyback operations to $4bn+ per operation effective Sept 9. That drains duration from the market, caps the long end, and raises dollar supply from the Treasury General Account. The 30-year came off a 19-year high; the 10-year sits at 4.65%. 🔴 Washington is explicitly prioritising lower long-term yields over dollar strength.
  • Fed — hawkish minutes, no market response: Funds rate 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh. The July 28–29 minutes released Wednesday read hawkish and the dollar fell anyway. September remains priced close to a hold; the Sept 15–16 decision carries the dot plot. 🔴 The rate story has stopped supporting the dollar even when it should.
  • ECB — September hike still near-consensus, now with an energy tailwind: Deposit rate 2.40%, roughly 84% priced for a 25bp hike on Sept 10 in Berlin. July HICP 2.9% (up from 2.8%), Q2 GDP +0.4% QoQ, unemployment 6.3%. European gas is elevated on Middle East supply shortages, keeping upside inflation risk live. 🔴 You pay for this on every transfer.
  • Strait of Hormuz — still disrupted, but no longer helping you: Transits are running 8–15 vessels/day against ~130 pre-conflict. An ADNOC-affiliated vessel was attacked Friday and the UAE blamed Iran; two more ADNOC ships were hit Thursday. Tankers are increasingly routing the Oman side under US naval protection, which weakens Iran's grip. Brent ~$91.5, WTI ~$85.8, EU gas €63.60/MWh. 🟡 The energy channel has finished repricing into the dollar and is now mostly a euro-inflation story that supports the ECB.
  • Rate differential — narrowing: Fed funds 3.75% vs ECB 2.40% — a 135bp policy gap that has not moved, but US 10Y 4.65% against Bunds at 3.26% is a ~139bp spread, in from ~150bp two weeks ago. 🔴 Treasury buybacks compress this mechanically, independent of anything the Fed does.

5. Two-Week Outlook

Base case: roughly €0.848 – €0.860 per dollar over the next two weeks — the whole band has shifted down about a cent again, and today's €0.8552 sits in the upper half of it rather than the middle. The skew is negative: the dollar is at a 2½-month low with momentum, September is priced as a hold, and the Treasury buyback is a structural rather than a cyclical drag. Your realistic upside is concentrated in two discretionary events eight days out — Warsh at Jackson Hole and the July PCE print — and neither is a coin flip in your favour.

APEX Take — Conversion Strategy

€ per $1 is €0.8552, down 0.15% on the day, 1.36% on the week, and at the lowest print of the entire six-month window — below the April trough of €0.8557 and 3.0% under the July 28 peak. The four-day "convert now" instruction was correct each day it was given, and the cost of ignoring it has compounded.

The recommendation changes today, and I want to be explicit that it changes. Telling you to dump 100% into the worst rate of the half-year is not analysis, it is momentum-chasing. Convert only what you actually need for near-term obligations — call it 40% — and hold the balance for the eight days to Jackson Hole (Aug 28) and July PCE (Aug 28), which are the only two scheduled events in the window with the weight to move you back toward €0.865. Hard deadline: convert the remaining 60% by Sept 9 regardless of level, ahead of the Sept 10 ECB decision, the Sept 15–16 FOMC and the start of Treasury's enlarged buyback operations. That is a dated rule, not a hope.

Why the balance of risk supports waiting a week and not a month: the drop was caused by a Treasury liquidity decision, not by US data deteriorating further — the July FOMC minutes actually read hawkish and were simply ignored. Flow-driven moves of this kind retrace more often than data-driven ones. But the Treasury buyback is a stated policy running from Sept 9, so the structural drag is real and does not expire. That asymmetry is exactly why the wait is capped at eight days with a hard date attached.

Standing recommendation for recurring transfers: you now have four consecutive sessions of being on the wrong side of timing decisions. Ask your bank's FX desk what a one-month forward on your typical conversion size costs. For a recurring, known-amount paycheck conversion, a forward removes this entire problem and is the cleaner instrument than any staging rule I can write for you.
