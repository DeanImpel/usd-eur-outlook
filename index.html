1. Bottom Line

Every dollar you convert today buys ~€0.870 — a small bounce (+0.2%) off yesterday, as the dollar clawed back part of Thursday's sharp sell-off, but still down about 0.9% on the week after the euro's two-day rally. This is a mediocre near-term window: you're getting less euro per dollar than a week ago, and both central banks now lean hawkish, so wait for the Aug 7 jobs report and Aug 12 CPI before committing size.
  • Six months ago (Feb) $1 got you as little as roughly €0.8455 on average. As of today, $1 gets you €0.8695 — up marginally from yesterday's €0.8675, but well off the €0.8807 six-month high set Jul 28.
  • The dollar recovered today: the Dollar Index sits at ~100.34, up 0.37% on the day, on short-covering after Thursday's sell-off. Over the past week, though, DXY is down roughly a point from the ~101.3 area — the dollar's weekly trend is still softer, which is why € per $1 is lower than it was seven days ago. EUR/USD traded around 1.1488 intraday, easing from Thursday's six-week high of 1.1537.
  • Both sides are now hiking-biased — a genuine two-way risk. Per CME FedWatch, traders price roughly a 66% chance the Fed hikes 25bp in September, with Cleveland's Hammack and Minneapolis's Kashkari both reinforcing hawkish views today. On the other side, euro-area July flash inflation came in at 2.9% YoY (up from 2.8%, core 2.5% from 2.4%), which Societe Generale reads as supporting another ECB hike in September. Whichever bank moves harder sets the direction.
  • The Strait of Hormuz crisis is now in its sixth month and day 152 of effective closure, with transits running near 10 ships/day against a normal baseline of roughly 88. Energy inflation in the euro area jumped to 10.0% YoY in July (from 8.5%) — the shock is now clearly feeding euro-area prices, and remains the single biggest swing factor for the dollar's safe-haven bid.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — bouncing, but softer on the week: The Dollar Index sits at ~100.34, up 0.37% today on short-covering after Thursday's sharp sell-off, yet down roughly 1% over the past week. 🟡 Today's move is favorable for you; the seven-day trend is not.
  • Fed — held at 3.50–3.75%, but a September hike is now the base case in markets: The July 29 decision was a 9–3 hold under Chair Kevin Warsh, with all three dissenters wanting an immediate ¼-point hike. Hammack said policy "is not restrictive enough"; Kashkari argued for a series of small moves rather than waiting. CME FedWatch now prices ~66% odds of a 25bp hike on Sept 15–16. 🟢 Rising US rate expectations support the dollar and mean more euro per dollar for you.
  • ECB — euro-area inflation firming, September hike expected: July flash HICP came in at 2.9% YoY (from 2.8%), core at 2.5% (from 2.4%), with energy at 10.0%. Societe Generale: the print, alongside a solid Q2 GDP figure, "should support another ECB rate hike in September" (Sept 9–10). 🔴 A hawkish ECB is euro-supportive and works against you — this is the main counterweight to the Fed story.
  • Strait of Hormuz — closed, day 152, no resolution: Transits are running near 10 ships/day vs. a ~88/day normal baseline, with the conflict now in its sixth month and US–Iran tensions escalating again. 🟢 Long-term dollar-supportive via the safe-haven bid — but a genuine ceasefire would unwind that premium quickly.
  • Energy pass-through — now visible in euro-area data: Euro-area energy inflation accelerated to 10.0% YoY in July from 8.5% in June. Societe Generale cautions that second-round effects "typically take time to pass through supply chains," so more pressure may still be coming. 🟡 Ambiguous: a cost drag on the euro-area economy, but it also pushes the ECB toward hiking, which lifts the euro.

APEX Take — Conversion Strategy

€ per $1 ticked up to €0.8695 today, +0.2% on the day, but you're getting about 0.9% less euro per dollar than a week ago and roughly 1.3% less than at the Jul 28 high of €0.8807. The setup changed this week in a way worth noting: it is no longer a one-sided Fed story. Markets price ~66% odds of a Fed hike in September (dollar-positive, good for you) at the same time that euro-area inflation firmed to 2.9% and analysts now expect an ECB hike in September too (euro-positive, bad for you). Those two forces largely offset — which is why the rate is chopping rather than trending.

Recommendation: Convert a modest tranche now — roughly 20–25% — since €0.8695 is comfortably above the six-month average and you're paid in dollars regardless. Hold the balance through the Aug 7 jobs report and Aug 12 CPI. If both run hot, September Fed hike odds climb, the dollar firms, and you should convert the bulk near or above €0.878. If they run soft, expect a drift toward €0.850–0.860 and hold rather than chase. Do not wait past mid-August in hopes of a better level: if the ECB September hike gets fully priced while the Fed's does not, this window will look good in hindsight. A limit order at €0.880 via your bank's FX desk captures the upside without you having to watch the screen.
