1. Bottom Line

Every dollar you convert today buys ~€0.868 — down from yesterday's €0.878 as the dollar weakened in the wake of the Fed's hold decision, despite three hawkish dissents favoring a hike. The broader trend since February remains in your favor (+3.7% vs. 6 months ago), but today's pullback is a reminder this isn't a one-way trade. With no major catalyst until the Aug 12 CPI print, hold off on converting a large lump sum and watch for a re-test of the recent €0.878–0.881 highs.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — weakened despite a hawkish-leaning Fed dissent: The Dollar Index sits at ~100.98, down from 101.30 yesterday, easing in the wake of the Fed's 9-3 hold decision. 🟡 Counterintuitive — the dissent (3 regional presidents wanting a hike) reads hawkish, but reduced Mideast risk premium and post-event profit-taking dominated today's price action.
  • Strait of Hormuz — still critical, structurally unresolved: Non-Iranian ship transits remain near 25 vessels vs. a 108 pre-crisis baseline (~90% YoY decline), and Houthi forces continue threatening a Saudi-port "maritime embargo" that could extend into the Red Sea. 🟢 long-term dollar-supportive — a sudden re-escalation or a real ceasefire could each move the rate quickly in either direction.
  • European gas prices — elevated, still near highs: TTF natural gas trades near €58/MWh, roughly flat vs. yesterday, after QatarEnergy extended its LNG force majeure for European buyers to end-September on Hormuz-linked supply fears. 🔴 for the euro — an ongoing cost drag on the eurozone economy.
  • ECB — off the board until September: The ECB held rates steady at its July meeting, striking a cautious "wait-and-see" tone; the ECB does not meet in August, so the next monetary policy decision is September 9–10, 2026. 🟡 Not a near-term catalyst, but worth tracking.
  • Fed — decision now known, held 9-3: The Fed held rates steady at 3.50–3.75% for a fifth consecutive meeting (announced Jul 29, 2:00pm ET); all three dissenters (Cleveland's Hammack, Minneapolis's Kashkari, Dallas's Logan) wanted a ¼-point hike. Year-end rate projections range 3.6%-4.1%. 🟡 The decision is now priced in — next FOMC is Sept 15-16.

APEX Take — Conversion Strategy

€ per $1 pulled back to €0.8677 today, down about 1.2% from yesterday's €0.8780, as the dollar weakened in the wake of the Fed's 9-3 hold decision — despite three hawkish dissents favoring a hike. You're still getting more euro per dollar than six months ago (+3.7%), but today's pullback is a reminder the trend isn't one-directional, and with the FOMC decision now behind us, the Aug 12 CPI print is the next real catalyst.

Recommendation: Hold off on converting a large lump sum right now — today's move is a pullback from a six-month high, not a new low. Wait for either a re-test of the recent €0.878–0.881 highs or the Aug 12 CPI print before committing more than a small tranche (10-20%). If CPI runs hot, convert the bulk of the remaining balance promptly; if it runs cool, expect the rate to soften further and hold for a better entry. Keep watching the Strait of Hormuz situation — it remains the single biggest swing factor over the next two weeks. As always, a forward contract or rate-alert/limit order via your bank's FX desk is a better tool than manual timing for the bulk of the balance.
