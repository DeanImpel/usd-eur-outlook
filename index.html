1. Bottom Line

🟡 Every dollar you convert today buys €0.8581 — up 0.08% on the day, a second consecutive small gain after Friday's six-month low of €0.8540, but still down 0.50% on the week and 0.71% below the March average. EUR/USD eased to 1.1654 as the euro consolidated below Friday's 1.1710 three-month high. This is a two-day drift higher inside a downtrend, not a reversal. The week's real information arrives Wednesday (core PCE) and Friday (Warsh at Jackson Hole); converting into a 0.08% move ahead of both is not a decision, it is impatience.
  • Six months ago (March) $1 bought roughly €0.8642 on average. Today $1 gets you €0.8581 — a 0.71% half-year loss. The €0.8807 peak of Jul 28 is still 2.6% above you; Friday's €0.8540 floor is 0.5% below.
  • The bounce is coming from euro consolidation, not dollar strength. EUR/USD backed off 1.1710 to 1.1654 on position-squaring, while the dollar index itself softened again on Tuesday as yields eased and oil retreated. Marginally favourable, but the mechanism is fragile — this is the euro pausing, not the dollar recovering.
  • ING has not moved off its dollar-negative call. Forecasts remain 1.17 for end-September and 1.18 for year-end — roughly €0.847–€0.855 per dollar, i.e. at or below today's spot. Chris Turner's framing is blunt: "most paths seem to lead to a weaker dollar." Fewer euro per dollar is still the house base case you are trading against.
  • Wednesday's core PCE is the first real test. Consensus is +0.2% m/m core with the annual rate holding at 3.3%, published alongside the second estimate of Q2 GDP. A hot print rebuilds the September hike case and is your cleanest realistic upside inside this window (more euro per dollar).
  • Friday is the event, and it is now doubly loaded. Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as Chair lands at 15:00 BST — in the same minute as the BLS payrolls benchmark revision. He has no track record as Chair, which widens the plausible reaction range in both directions. That collision is precisely why the Sept 9 deadline on your remaining tranche sits where it does.
  • The ECB leg has not cheapened. Deposit rate 2.40%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin, a 25bp hike still above 90% priced after July HICP at 2.9%. Euro-area composite PMI at a nine-month high and EU gas back up +4.0% keep the hawkish channel open. Fewer euro per dollar on policy and flow alike.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — soft again after a one-day pause: DXY sits near 98.85 after printing 98.99 Monday and a three-month low of 98.55 on Aug 22. The index is roughly 2.7% below the late-July high near 101.60 and has weakened 2.5% over the past month. Tuesday's softness came from easing US yields and retreating oil, with the Fed in focus ahead of PCE. 🔴 The single largest driver of your rate resumed falling after one session of stabilisation.
  • US Treasury — the structural drag remains the story: the decision to at least double buybacks of longer-dated debt (10y–20y and 20y–30y lifted to $4bn+ per operation, effective Sept 9 through Nov 4) is what took the shine off the greenback, not the data. US 10Y at 4.70%. Thursday brings a $44bn 7-year auction as the live test. 🔴 Washington is explicitly prioritising lower long-term yields over dollar strength.
  • Fed — hawkish committee, untested chair, Friday is the event: funds rate 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh, sworn in 22 May 2026; three regional presidents dissented for hikes in July. Decision Sept 15–16 with the dot plot. The Jackson Hole keynote on Friday Aug 28 is his debut in the role and lands 19 days before the decision, in the same minute as the BLS payrolls benchmark revision. 🟢 Your single best scheduled chance at a better rate inside this window — and the widest error bar.
  • ECB — September hike still above 90% priced: deposit rate 2.40%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin (Governing Council Sept 9–10, Bundesbank-hosted). July HICP 2.9% headline; euro-area unemployment 6.3%; composite PMI at a nine-month high with manufacturing the strongest in four years. EU gas €68.48/MWh (+4.0%) keeps the upside inflation channel open. 🔴 The expensive half of your trade, and it is not getting cheaper.
  • Iran sanctions — package delivered, oil sold the news: Bessent's "economic D-Day" landed and crude went down, not up: WTI ~$85.16 (−2.2%), Brent ~$92.22 (−2.3%). 🟡 The escalation-equals-dollar-bid reflex did not fire. Watch instead for whether enforcement catches Chinese buyers of Iranian crude — that is the path ING flags as dollar negative because it reopens the tariff war.
  • Strait of Hormuz — day 177, throughput at 20% of pre-war: transits are running at roughly one-fifth of the pre-war average per UK maritime agency figures. Iran has published a blacklist of 46 vessels facing fines, detention or seizure, and security chief Mohsen Rezaei has threatened to halt outbound oil entirely if neighbours join the US campaign. Some operators are running dark, AIS off, at night. 🟡 Still ambiguous: haven flow bids the dollar, but the same shock feeds euro-area energy inflation and ECB hawkishness. The two channels have been cancelling for weeks.
  • Rate differential — still the dollar's unused argument: Fed funds 3.75% vs ECB 2.40% is a 135bp policy gap; US 10Y 4.70% against Bunds at 3.25% is a ~145bp spread. 🟢 On carry alone the dollar is cheap here. Flow, fiscal credibility and policy signalling — not rate differentials — have run this tape since late July.

5. Two-Week Outlook

Base case: roughly €0.852 – €0.866 per dollar over the next two weeks. Today's €0.8581 sits mid-band. The band is unchanged in width from yesterday but has shifted marginally higher with spot; the skew is still against you. ING's official forecasts of 1.17 end-September and 1.18 end-year imply €0.847–€0.855 — at or below today's spot. This remains a range-hold case with one credible upside trigger (Warsh, Friday) and three credible downside triggers (a soft PCE, a tailing 7-year auction, a hot euro-area HICP on Sep 1).

This is not financial advice. FX markets are volatile and short-term moves are inherently unpredictable — treat the ranges above as scenario framing, not a guarantee. APEX Take — Conversion Strategy

€ per $1 is €0.8581, up 0.08% on the day and a second consecutive small gain — but still down 0.50% on the week and only 0.5% above Friday's six-month low of €0.8540. Two quiet up-days ahead of the two events that actually matter is not a signal. Nothing about the plan changes today.

Hold the staged position: 40% converted, 60% held, hard deadline Sept 9. If you executed the 40% tranche, do nothing today. If you have converted nothing yet, convert 40% now for near-term obligations and hold the balance. The remaining 60% converts by Sept 9 regardless of level — ahead of the Sept 10 ECB decision, the Sept 15–16 FOMC, and the start of Treasury's enlarged buybacks on Sept 9. That is a dated rule, not a forecast, and it is what keeps this from becoming an open-ended bet.

The tactical trigger stands, and this is the week it can fire: if the rate trades above €0.863 at any point between Wednesday's PCE and Friday's Warsh keynote, convert the remaining 60% into that strength rather than waiting for Sept 9. That is the top of the base band and the level ING's own forecasts say you should not expect to see again this quarter. Set a rate alert at €0.863 today. Friday's keynote and the payrolls benchmark revision land in the same minute — you will not have time to react manually.

Standing recommendation for recurring transfers: the honest scoreboard is that timing has cost you roughly 2.6% against the July 28 peak. Ask your bank's FX desk what a one-month forward on your typical conversion size costs. For a recurring, known-amount paycheck conversion, a forward removes this entire problem and is a cleaner instrument than any staging rule I can write for you.
