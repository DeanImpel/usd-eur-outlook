1. Bottom Line

🔴 Every dollar you convert today buys ~€0.854 — a second consecutive six-month low, down 0.28% on the day and 0.94% on the week as the euro printed a fresh three-month high at 1.1710. But the picture changed at 9:45am ET: the US flash composite PMI came in at 56.0 against a consensus near 54, the strongest reading since April 2022, with services at 56.8 versus 54.0 expected. That is the first genuine dollar-positive data surprise of this entire slide, it lands with EUR/USD's RSI at 74.8 (overbought), and it materially improves the case for holding the balance of your conversion to the Aug 28 double event rather than capitulating at the low.
  • Six months ago (March) $1 bought roughly €0.8642 on average. Today $1 gets you €0.8540 — a 1.18% half-year loss and a new floor for the window. The €0.8807 peak of Jul 28 is now 3.1% above you.
  • The US PMI beat is the tell worth acting on. Composite 56.0 (from 54.5), services 56.8 (from 54.6, consensus 54.0), fastest services pace since December 2024, employment growing at the quickest rate since early 2025 and business confidence at a nine-month high. More euro per dollar if it holds — the "soft US data" narrative that carried EUR/USD from 1.14 to 1.17 just took a direct hit.
  • The euro leg, by contrast, was only a modest beat. Euro-area flash composite 52.1 (from 52.0) — a nine-month high, but a tenth of a point. Manufacturing output 53.4 was the strong leg; services was flat at 51.7. On a like-for-like read, the US composite is running roughly 4 points above the euro area. That is a growth differential the market has been ignoring.
  • The Treasury buyback remains the structural drag and has not gone away. Washington will at least double long-dated buyback operations to $4bn+ per operation from September 9 through November 4. Lower long-end yields plus more dollar liquidity out of the TGA is a stated policy stance, not a data point. Fewer euro per dollar, and it does not mean-revert on one good PMI.
  • The ECB leg has got more expensive, not less. Market pricing for a 25bp hike on Sept 10 has firmed to above 90% (DBS: 26bp priced), up from ~84% a week ago, after July HICP confirmed at 2.9% headline and 2.5% core. September FOMC hike odds sit near 39%. Fewer euro per dollar — you pay this on every transfer regardless of what US data does.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — three-month low, but the reason for it just weakened: DXY sits near 98.67, down 0.17%, after setting a three-month low at 98.56 on Thursday. The index has lost roughly 2.9% from the late-July high near 101.60. 🔴 Still the single largest driver of your rate and still trending against you — but today's PMI beat is the first credible argument for a bounce off this level.
  • US activity data — a genuine upside surprise: Flash composite 56.0, services 56.8, manufacturing softer with goods output at a 13-month low on supply disruption and destocking. The services-led beat is the important part: it is the two-thirds of the economy that drives the inflation the Fed watches. 🟢 The clearest favourable development for you in three weeks.
  • US Treasury — the structural drag, unchanged: Buyback operations in the 10y–20y and 20y–30y sectors at least double to $4bn+ per operation effective Sept 9, running to Nov 4. Notably, the operation that triggered this saw $20bn offered against $2bn taken — the cap, not demand, was the binding constraint. US 10Y sits at 4.71%. 🔴 Washington is explicitly prioritising lower long-term yields over dollar strength.
  • Fed — split committee, opaque chair: Funds rate 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh. Roughly half of FOMC participants penciled in hikes for 2026 at Warsh's first meeting in June, and three regional presidents dissented in favour of hikes in July — unusually high dissent this early in a chair's tenure. September hike odds near 39%; decision Sept 15–16 with the dot plot. 🟡 A hawkish committee against a chair who has told reporters he intends to say very little.
  • ECB — September hike now above 90% priced: Deposit rate 2.40%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin. July HICP confirmed at 2.9% headline / 2.5% core, both in line. Euro-area unemployment 6.3%, flash composite PMI at a nine-month high. European gas at €65.20/MWh (+2.9%) keeps upside inflation risk live. 🔴 The euro leg is the expensive half of this trade and it is getting more expensive.
  • Strait of Hormuz — day 173, effectively closed, and the sign has flipped against you: Commercial transits have collapsed (as few as 1 vessel on Aug 16 against ~73/day pre-conflict), but the US military is running a protected corridor moving roughly 10m bbl/day, about half pre-war volume. Iran conditions any reopening on sanctions relief and reparations. Brent ~$93.4 (+1.9%), WTI ~$86.4 (+2.4%). 🟡 The energy channel now feeds euro-area inflation and ECB hawkishness roughly as much as it bids the dollar.
  • Rate differential — the gap the market is not paying for: Fed funds 3.75% vs ECB 2.40% — a 135bp policy gap. US 10Y 4.71% against Bunds at 3.26% is a ~145bp spread, which has actually widened back out from ~139bp. 🟢 On rate differential alone the dollar is cheap here; flow and policy signalling, not carry, are running this tape.

5. Two-Week Outlook

Base case: roughly €0.848 – €0.862 per dollar over the next two weeks. The band is essentially unchanged from yesterday but the skew has improved for the first time in two weeks: today's €0.8540 sits at the bottom of it, EUR/USD's RSI is at 74.8 and stretched, and the US composite PMI at 56.0 is a hard data point against the narrative that drove the move. The offsetting drag is real — the Sept 10 ECB hike is above 90% priced and the Treasury buyback expands Sept 9 — which is why this is a bounce case, not a trend-reversal case.

More euro per dollar

€0.860 – €0.872

Today's PMI beat holds through the New York close, core PCE runs hot on Aug 28, and Warsh acknowledges the activity strength at Jackson Hole. The overbought RSI unwind alone is worth a cent. Sept 4 payrolls confirming the PMI hiring signal is what takes you to the top of this band.

Range-bound (most likely)

€0.848 – €0.862

The PMI beat produces a one-cent bounce that stalls, Warsh stays on the symposium theme and says nothing usable, PCE lands in line, and the Sept 10 ECB hike keeps a floor under the euro. EUR/USD chops between 1.1600 and 1.1790. You get a better entry than today but not a good one.

Fewer euro per dollar

€0.838 – €0.848

The PMI beat is faded within a session, cool PCE flips the market from hold to pricing a Q4 cut, euro-area HICP surprises high, or the enlarged Sept 9 buyback programme is read as an outright yield cap. Any one path alone takes you to a new six-month low below today's.

This is not financial advice. FX markets are volatile and short-term moves are inherently unpredictable — treat the ranges above as scenario framing, not a guarantee. For a transaction of meaningful size, consider consulting a licensed financial advisor or your bank's treasury/FX desk, especially for hedging tools (forward contracts, limit orders) that remove timing risk entirely.
