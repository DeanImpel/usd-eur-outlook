1. Bottom Line

🟡 The level held. Friday's gain is intact and your trigger is still live. Every dollar buys €0.8622 this morning — 0.13% below Friday's settled €0.8633, which is noise, not a reversal — and +0.57% on the week. EUR/USD is 1.1598, still the weakest euro since Aug 19. The instruction from Friday stands: convert the remaining balance today. The one genuinely new fact is that Hormuz de-escalation has reversed — US forces struck Iranian rocket launchers preparing to mine the waterway on Sunday, ending weeks of calm and sending Brent up ~3% to roughly $90.7. That is euro-negative and therefore helps you, but it is a war headline, not a trend, and the scheduled calendar from here — euro-area HICP tomorrow, the ECB hike on Sep 10 — still leans against you.
  • Six months ago (March) $1 bought roughly €0.8642 on average. Today $1 gets you €0.8622 — a 0.23% half-year loss. The €0.8807 peak of Jul 28 is 2.10% above you; the €0.8540 floor of Aug 21 is 0.96% below. You remain in the upper half of the six-month range.

That is inside the daily noise band and, critically, it did not give back Friday's 0.60% Warsh repricing. Six of the last eight sessions have printed between €0.854 and €0.864 — a 1.2% box.
  • Hormuz has turned back the other way, and that is the day's real news. US Central Command confirmed strikes on Sunday against Iranian rocket launchers preparing to send mines into the strait, ending weeks of relative calm and directly undercutting the Aug 26 Iran–Oman interim two-lane route. Brent jumped to roughly $90.7 (+2.9%), WTI to $86.2 (+3.4%), and EU natural gas gapped +4.9%. Favourable for you — the euro area is the net energy importer in this pair.

Around 6–8 million barrels a day are still transiting the strait, and the same escalate-then-negotiate pattern has now repeated four times since March. An energy spike that gives you a better rate on Monday can be fully retraced by Wednesday on one Qatari or Omani headline. This is volatility, not a durable dollar bid.
  • The Fed leg is unchanged and still the strongest thing you have. Warsh's Jackson Hole debut — inflation has not meaningfully slowed, the 2% target "firm and fixed," the Fed still has "work to do" — left Sept 16 hike odds near 50%, up from ~35% pre-speech. He gave no forward guidance, which is why this repriced the dollar once rather than starting a trend. Favourable.
  • The euro's hawkish ammunition is still unspent, and it fires this week. French EU-harmonised inflation at 2.7% and Spain at 4.5% (highest since 2023) have markets pricing the ECB deposit rate at 2.80% by March, from 2.25% now, with roughly a 60% chance of eventually reaching 3%. German CPI lands today, euro-area flash HICP tomorrow, and the ECB decides on Sep 10. Fewer euro per dollar when that fires.
  • The calendar in front of you is dollar-negative on balance. Aug 31 German CPI, Sep 1 euro-area flash HICP, Sep 4 US payrolls, Sep 9 Treasury's enlarged buybacks begin and the ECB convenes in Berlin, Sep 10 the ECB decision, Sep 11 US CPI. 3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — Friday's break is holding, not extending: DXY 99.55, −0.15% on the day after Friday's settled 99.70 close. That is still roughly +0.9% on the week from the Aug 24 base near 98.7, and the index remains above its 200-day moving average after clearing it Friday. It sits below the 101.4–101.6 late-July high and about +1.3% over 12 months. 🟢 Consolidation above a broken level is constructive; it is not yet a trend.
  • Fed — hawkish chair, hawkish committee, no guidance: funds rate 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh (sworn in 22 May 2026); three regional presidents dissented for hikes in July. Warsh leaned hawkish on substance at Jackson Hole while explicitly declining to signal September and calling for a "quieter" central bank. Sept 16 hike odds ~50%, up from ~35%. Decision Sept 15–16 with the dot plot. 🟢 Still the most dollar-positive item on this page.
  • US Treasury — the structural drag arrives Sept 9: the doubling of long-dated buybacks (10y–20y and 20y–30y lifted to $4bn+ per operation, effective Sept 9 through Nov 4) is what took the shine off the greenback through late August. 🔴 A standing negative that has not started yet — Washington is prioritising lower long-term yields over dollar strength.
  • ECB — hawkish repricing intact, and about to be tested: policy rate 2.40%, deposit rate 2.25%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin (Governing Council meets Sept 9–10, hosted by the Bundesbank; announcement 14:15 CET). A September hike is nearly fully priced; markets hold 2.80% by March and roughly a 60% chance of eventually reaching 3%, with about 40bp of additional tightening priced by year-end. Euro-area July HICP 2.9%; unemployment 6.3%. 🔴 German CPI today and euro-area flash HICP tomorrow are the first live tests.
  • Strait of Hormuz — day 183, and the de-escalation has broken down: US Central Command confirmed strikes on Sunday Aug 30 against Iranian rocket launchers preparing to lay mines in the waterway, ending weeks of calm and calling into question the 60-day interim two-lane route Iran and Oman agreed on Aug 26. Roughly 6–8 mb/d still transits without a US–Iran peace agreement. 🟢 For the first time in three weeks this line is favourable to you — but it is the most reversible item on the page, and a renewed reopening push flips it straight back.
  • Oil — the reopening trade unwound in one session: Brent ~$90.7 (+2.9%) and WTI ~$86.2 (+3.4%), recovering most of last week's 5% decline; EU natural gas +4.9%. 🟢 Every dollar on Brent is a euro-area terms-of-trade hit and therefore more euro per dollar for you. The mirror risk is unchanged: a genuine reopening drives Brent to the $70s and takes you back through the €0.8540 floor.
  • Rate differential — the dollar's structural argument: Fed funds 3.75% vs ECB 2.40% is a 135bp policy gap; US 10Y around 4.72% against Bunds at 3.30% is a ~142bp spread, a touch narrower than Friday as Bunds sold off on the inflation prints. US CPI 3.4% vs euro-area 2.9%. 🟢 Carry still favours the dollar, but the spread is compressing at the margin — that is the mechanism by which the ECB story eats your rate.
