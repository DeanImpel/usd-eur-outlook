1. Bottom Line

🔴 Every dollar you convert today buys ~€0.867 — down another 0.2% on the day and 1.4% below last week, as the euro pushed to its highest level since mid-June just above $1.15. This is a poor near-term window and it is deteriorating on schedule: Qatar confirmed an interim US–Iran proposal is drafted, oil has now lost more than 10% in three sessions, and September Fed hike odds have slid to roughly 54–56% from ~81% on Aug 3 — the support that was holding € per $1 up is being priced out in real time.
  • Six months ago (March) $1 bought roughly €0.8642 on average. Today $1 gets you €0.8671 — a net gain of about a third of a percent over half a year, and 1.5% below the €0.8807 six-month high set on Jul 28.
  • The dollar is grinding lower, not crashing. The Dollar Index sits at ~99.87, −0.03% on the day and down roughly 1.6% on the week from late-July highs near 101.50 — its worst weekly run in three months. EUR/USD trades near 1.1533, up 0.20% today and +0.80% over the past month.
  • The Fed leg has broken. Market pricing for a September 15–16 hike has fallen to roughly 54–56% (Kalshi 54%, CME FedWatch 55.9%), down from ~65% yesterday and ~81% on Aug 3. Two sessions have removed roughly 25 points of hike probability. The oil-driven inflation impulse that justified the hike is gone.
  • Hormuz: an interim proposal is now on the table. Qatar said mediators drafted an interim framework and both Washington and Tehran signalled progress; Treasury Secretary Bessent said a reopening deal could land as soon as today or tomorrow. Brent has fallen below $79 (~$78.81, −5.9%) and WTI toward $75 (~$75.26, −6.3%) after losing more than 10% across the prior two sessions.
  • The euro's own bid keeps hardening. Q2 euro-area GDP grew 0.4% (double consensus, fastest since early 2025) and July HICP firmed to 2.9% YoY with core and services both accelerating. An ECB hike to 2.50% on Sept 10 remains largely priced. Meanwhile US June CPI cooled to 3.5% from 4.2% and June payrolls printed a weak 57K. The rate differential is compressing from both ends — against you.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — soft and drifting lower: DXY at ~99.87, essentially flat today (−0.03%) but down ~1.6% on the week and roughly 1.6 points off the late-July peak near 101.50. The index has now spent three sessions below 100 with no bounce. 🔴 Unfavorable for you — fewer euro per dollar than at any point since mid-June.
  • Fed — September hike odds collapsed to ~54–56% from ~81% in two sessions: The July 29 decision was a 9–3 hold at 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh, with three dissenters wanting an immediate ¼-point hike. With Brent down more than 10% in three days and June CPI already cooling to 3.5%, the energy-inflation case Warsh cited is unwinding. Next decision Sept 15–16, with the dot plot. 🔴 This has flipped fully from your strongest support to your largest active drag.
  • Strait of Hormuz — interim proposal drafted, deal possible within days: Qatar confirmed mediators produced an interim framework; Bessent flagged a possible agreement today or tomorrow. The working terms remain full reopening with no Iranian transit tolls for at least 60 days. Brent ~$78.81, WTI ~$75.26. 🔴 Each step toward reopening strips the dollar's safe-haven premium and its inflation impulse at the same time.
  • ECB — hiking bias intact, Sept 10 largely priced: Deposit rate at 2.40%; Q2 GDP +0.4%, July HICP 2.9% YoY with core and services firming, unemployment steady at 6.3%. Falling oil has trimmed but not removed hike expectations. Next decision Sept 10. 🔴 A hawkish, accelerating euro area works directly against € per $1.
  • Energy pass-through — cutting against you on both sides: Cheaper crude relieves Europe's growth constraint more than the US, while simultaneously removing the US inflation impulse that supported the Fed hike trade. 🔴 The same catalyst hurts you twice.
