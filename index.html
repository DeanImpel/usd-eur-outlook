1. Bottom Line

Every dollar you convert today buys ~€0.877 — a small pullback from Friday's close as the dollar eased slightly (DXY -0.28%), but still near the best levels of the past year, with the euro trading close to its own one-year low against the dollar. The broader trend since February remains solidly in your favor (+3.7% vs. 6 months ago), and with the FOMC decision now just 2 days away (Jul 28–29), this is still a reasonable window to convert a moderate tranche now while staging the rest around the Fed decision.
  • Six months ago (Feb) $1 got you as little as roughly €0.8455 on average. As of today, $1 gets you €0.8774 — comfortably above the 6-month average, though today's tick is a modest ~0.2% pullback from Friday's €0.8791 close and still short of June 24's high of €0.8806.
  • The dollar is rangebound rather than trending: the Dollar Index sits at ~101.1, down slightly today after topping a one-month high of 101.5 on July 23 (July range: 98.71–102.52), as EUR/USD ticked up to ~1.1397 — the euro remains near its one-year low against the dollar.
  • The Strait of Hormuz crisis remains critical and unresolved: non-Iranian ship transits are still running near 25 vessels versus a pre-crisis baseline of 108 (~90% YoY decline), and Iran-backed Houthis continue to threaten a "maritime embargo" on ships calling at Saudi ports. This is reinforcing dollar safe-haven demand — favorable for you.
  • European natural gas (TTF) remains elevated near €58–63/MWh, its highest levels since January 2023, driven directly by the Hormuz-related LNG supply squeeze — a growing structural cost drag on the eurozone economy and a modest headwind for the euro over time.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — choppy, rangebound, easing slightly: The Dollar Index sits at ~101.1, down 0.28% today after topping a one-month high of 101.5 on July 23, caught between a Hormuz-driven war-risk premium and a Fed that remains cautious/divided on rate-cut timing. 🟡 Mixed — not a strong signal in either direction right now.
  • Strait of Hormuz — still critical, not stabilizing: Non-Iranian ship transits remain near 25 vessels vs. a 108 pre-crisis baseline (~90% YoY decline), and Houthi forces continue threatening a Saudi-port "maritime embargo" that could extend into the Red Sea. 🟢 The dominant driver right now — ongoing tension typically supports the dollar via safe-haven flows, though a sudden ceasefire could reverse this quickly.
  • European gas prices — elevated: TTF natural gas remains near €58–63/MWh, its highest levels since January 2023, on reduced Persian Gulf LNG flows tied to the Hormuz crisis. 🔴 for the euro — a real and growing cost drag on the eurozone economy.
  • ECB — off the board until September: The ECB's last Governing Council meeting was July 23 (held steady after the June 11 hike to a 2.25% deposit rate); the ECB does not meet in August, so the next monetary policy decision is expected September 10, 2026. 🟡 Not a near-term catalyst, but worth tracking.
  • Fed — the dominant near-term catalyst, 2 days away: Fed meets Jul 28–29, decision due Jul 29 at 2:00pm ET. A hawkish hold/statement would be favorable for you; any dovish pivot on rate-cut timing would work against further gains. 🟡 The key event risk over the next 48 hours.

APEX Take — Conversion Strategy

€ per $1 pulled back modestly today to €0.8774, down ~0.2% from Friday's close, as a choppy/rangebound dollar eased slightly even as Hormuz-driven safe-haven demand keeps it near multi-month highs. You're still getting meaningfully more euro per dollar than you were six months ago (+3.7%), and with the FOMC decision now just 2 days out (Jul 28–29), there's a real chance the rate moves outside the current range within the week.

Recommendation: Convert a modest tranche now (e.g., 15–20%) at ~€0.877 per dollar, and hold the bulk to convert around or shortly after the July 29 FOMC decision — a hawkish Fed outcome would argue for converting the remainder promptly, while a dovish pivot argues for staging the rest more slowly and watching for a better print. Keep an eye on the Strait of Hormuz situation in the meantime — a sudden ceasefire is the single biggest risk to the current favorable trend. If your bank or transfer provider offers a forward contract or rate alert/limit order, that remains a better tool than manual timing for the remaining balance.
