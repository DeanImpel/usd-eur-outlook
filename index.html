1. Bottom Line

Every dollar you convert today buys ~€0.868 — down about 0.2% from Friday and roughly 1.3% below where it stood a week ago, as the dollar sold off on the weekend Iran de-escalation headlines and coordinated US–Japan yen support. This has become a poor near-term window and the risk is now skewed against you: if the Hormuz reopening deal actually lands, the dollar loses both its safe-haven bid and the energy-inflation story propping up September Fed hike odds, which would push € per $1 lower still.
  • Six months ago (March) $1 bought roughly €0.8642 on average. Today $1 gets you €0.8679 — barely changed over half a year, and down about 1.5% from the €0.8807 six-month high set on Jul 28.
  • The dollar is soft. The Dollar Index sits at ~99.72, down 0.19% on the day and roughly 1.5% lower on the week — its worst weekly showing in three months, and now down about 1.1% over the past month. EUR/USD trades near 1.1522, back above the 1.15 handle and close to its highest since mid-June.
  • The Iran story flipped this weekend, and it flipped against you. Trump announced Aug 2 that he had cancelled a planned strike on Iran after "parameters of a deal" were agreed — reportedly including the complete reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and an end to Iran's nuclear program, in exchange for the US lifting its naval blockade. That unwinds the dollar's crisis premium. Caveat: Al Jazeera reports no actual breakthrough on how Hormuz traffic would be managed, and Iran's acting defence minister dismissed the pullback as "psychological operations."
  • The Fed is the one thing still working in your favor. CME FedWatch has priced roughly an 81% chance of a 25bp hike on Sept 15–16 — up from below 53% a week earlier — after the July 29 9–3 hold at 3.50–3.75% with three dissents demanding an immediate hike. But those odds were built on the oil-driven inflation impulse. A genuine Hormuz reopening cuts oil, cuts that impulse, and takes the hike odds down with it.
  • Against that, the euro has its own tailwinds: Q2 euro-area GDP grew 0.4%, double the forecast, and July flash HICP firmed to 2.9% YoY (core 2.5%), keeping a September ECB hike live for the Sept 9–10 meeting. Euro-area energy inflation at 10.0% YoY means the euro bloc is also the bigger beneficiary of any Hormuz reopening.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — broadly weaker, and weakening for two separate reasons: DXY at ~99.72, down 0.19% today and ~1.5% on the week. Two drivers: confirmed coordinated US–Japan intervention to support the yen, which is dollar-negative across the board, and the risk-on unwind from the Iran de-escalation headlines. 🔴 Unfavorable for you — fewer euro per dollar.
  • Fed — held at 3.50–3.75%, but September hike odds are now near 81%: The July 29 decision was a 9–3 hold under Chair Kevin Warsh, the fifth consecutive hold, with all three dissenters wanting an immediate ¼-point hike. CME FedWatch prices roughly 81% odds of a 25bp hike on Sept 15–16 (0% chance of a cut, ~19% unchanged), up sharply from below 53% the prior week on the back of surging oil. 🟢 Favorable for you — but this is the piece most exposed to the Hormuz news.
  • Strait of Hormuz — deal "parameters" agreed, strike cancelled, but no mechanism yet: Trump halted the planned attack on Aug 2 citing agreed parameters including full reopening of the Strait. Saudi Arabia, Qatar, the UAE, Turkey and Pakistan all pressed for de-escalation. But Iran denies talks are scheduled with the US and has routed discussions through Oman; no framework exists for managing transit. Closure is now in day ~155. 🔴 De-escalation strips the dollar's safe-haven premium — the single biggest negative for you right now.
  • ECB — euro-area data firming, September hike still live: Q2 GDP came in at +0.4%, double consensus. July flash HICP at 2.9% YoY (from 2.8%), core 2.5%, energy 10.0%. Next decision Sept 9–10 in Berlin. 🔴 A hawkish, growing euro area is euro-supportive and works directly against you.
  • Energy pass-through — the swing variable in both directions: Euro-area energy inflation at 10.0% YoY is a bigger drag on the euro bloc than on the US, so a Hormuz reopening helps Europe disproportionately. 🔴 Net: reopening is euro-positive on growth and dollar-negative on both safe-haven and rate-differential grounds — a double hit to € per $1.

APEX Take — Conversion Strategy

€ per $1 slipped to €0.8679, down 0.2% on the day and 1.3% on the week. More important than the move is the change in the risk balance. Through July, the Hormuz closure was quietly working for you on two fronts: a safe-haven dollar bid, and an oil-driven inflation impulse that drove September Fed hike odds from 53% to 81%. Trump's Aug 2 cancellation of the Iran strike puts both of those in play at once. If a reopening actually happens, oil falls, the crisis premium evaporates, the Fed hike trade unwinds, and Europe — the bigger energy importer — benefits disproportionately. That is a triple negative for € per $1, and it is the reason the downside scenario now carries more weight than the upside.

Recommendation: Convert a larger tranche now — roughly 40–50%, up from the 20–25% guidance last week. The rationale is asymmetry, not level: €0.8679 is unremarkable, but you are being paid to take risk off ahead of a headline you cannot time. Hold the balance through the Aug 7 jobs report and Aug 12 CPI, and place a limit order at €0.878 with your bank's FX desk to catch a bounce if the Iran talks stall. If a Hormuz reopening is formally announced, do not wait for a better level — convert the remainder inside 48 hours, because the repricing will be fast. If instead the talks visibly break down and strikes return to the table, hold the balance into the Sept 15–16 FOMC.
