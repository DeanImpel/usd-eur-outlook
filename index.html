3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — the break is confirmed on settled data: DXY closed at 99.70, up 0.55% on the day — a firmer print than the 99.47/+0.31% provisional read. That is roughly +1.2% on the week from the Aug 22 low of 98.55, the index's first weekly gain in over a month, and it clears the 200-day moving average decisively en route to the 100-DMA. It is still below the 101.4–101.6 late-July high but up ~1.4% over 12 months. 🟢 Six sessions of base-building resolved upward, and the settled close validates it.
  • Fed — hawkish chair, hawkish committee, no guidance: funds rate 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh (sworn in 22 May 2026); three regional presidents dissented for hikes in July. Warsh's Jackson Hole debut leaned hawkish on substance — inflation has not meaningfully slowed, the 2% target "firm and fixed," the Fed will "have work to do" — while declining to signal September. Sept 16 hike odds ~50%, up from ~35%. Decision Sept 15–16 with the dot plot. 🟢 Still the most dollar-positive item on this page.
  • US Treasury — the structural drag returns Sept 9: the doubling of long-dated buybacks (10y–20y and 20y–30y lifted to $4bn+ per operation, effective Sept 9 through Nov 4) is what took the shine off the greenback through late August. Friday's rally recovered that drawdown in the index. 🔴 Still a standing negative — Washington is prioritising lower long-term yields over dollar strength, and the operations have not started yet.
  • ECB — hawkish repricing intact, and unspent: policy rate 2.40%, deposit rate 2.25%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin (Governing Council meets Sept 9–10, hosted by the Bundesbank; announcement 14:15 CET). A September hike is nearly fully priced; markets hold 2.80% by March and roughly a 60% chance of eventually reaching 3%, with traders pricing about 40bp of additional tightening by year-end. Euro-area July HICP 2.9%; unemployment 6.3%. 🔴 The euro still fell on Friday — that bid is waiting to be spent on Sep 1 and Sep 10.
  • Strait of Hormuz — day 182, and the corridor is signed: Iran and Oman agreed a 60-day extendable interim route on Aug 26 — northbound inbound lane in Iranian waters, southbound outbound lane in Omani waters — with technical talks planned on information sharing and navigational/security services. Qatar's PM added a third mediator track in Tehran on Aug 27. Full reopening remains tied to US commitments under the June interim deal, which has lapsed, and Washington says it is not currently talking to Tehran. 🔴 This has moved from talks to a live arrangement. A full reopening is a euro-positive terms-of-trade shock and remains the main unhedged risk to your conversion.
  • Oil — a week of losses, then a floor: Brent ~$88.29 (−0.26% on the day) after a week down more than 5% on improving flow expectations; WTI ~$83.44. 🔴 Direction of travel is lower and every dollar off Brent is a euro-area windfall. A break into the $70s on a genuine reopening is the fastest route back through your €0.8540 floor.
  • Rate differential — the dollar's argument, now being used: Fed funds 3.75% vs ECB 2.40% is a 135bp policy gap; US 10Y around 4.72% against Bunds at 3.27% is a ~145bp spread. US CPI 3.4% vs euro-area 2.9%. 🟢 Friday was the first session since late July in which carry, not flow and fiscal positioning, set the price. If that persists it is worth considerably more than 0.60%.
