1. Bottom Line

🟢 Warsh delivered, and it went your way. Every dollar now buys €0.8614 — up 0.38% on the day, up 0.87% on the week, the best level since Aug 19 and the first genuine weekly gain in your direction since July. The half-year deficit has narrowed from 0.61% to just 0.32%. This is the best window you have had in three weeks, and your €0.863 trigger is now 0.2% away. Markets are closed until the Sydney open Sunday evening — set the order now, because the next two weeks (euro-area HICP Sep 1, ECB Sep 10) lean against you.
  • Six months ago (March) $1 bought roughly €0.8642 on average. Today $1 gets you €0.8614 — a 0.32% half-year loss, down from 0.61% before Friday's move. The €0.8807 peak of Jul 28 is 2.24% above you; the €0.8540 floor of Aug 21 is 0.87% below.
  • The keynote was the hawkish half of the range. Warsh said recent inflation readings, while "better than expected," do not tell him "underlying trends have meaningfully improved," that the 2% target is "firm and fixed," and — critically — that financial conditions are not currently restrictive. He said the Fed would "have work to do" if inflation does not cool. Market-implied odds of a 25bp hike at the Sept 16 FOMC jumped to roughly 50%, from about 35% before he spoke. Favourable.
  • He also refused to give guidance, and that caps the upside. Warsh called for a "quieter" central bank and said investors should not be "looking primarily to the Fed for their next trade." He stopped well short of signalling a September increase. So the dollar got a repricing, not a regime change — which is why this is a 0.38% move rather than the 1.5% tail. Do not extrapolate Friday into next week.
  • The benchmark revision was a non-event and quietly helped. The BLS preliminary benchmark marked March 2026 payrolls down 79,000 (−0.1%) — half the 10-year average absolute revision of 0.2%, and far smaller than the market feared. The downside risk on the calendar cleared without doing damage.
  • The ECB leg is still the expensive half and it did not soften. French EU-harmonised inflation at 2.7% (three-month high) and Spain at 4.5% (highest since 2023) still have markets pricing the deposit rate at 2.80% by March and roughly a 60% chance of eventually reaching 3%. The euro fell on Friday despite that data, not because of it — meaning the dollar did all the work and the euro's hawkish bid is still loaded, waiting for Sept 1 and Sept 10. Fewer euro per dollar if that bid gets confirmation.
  • Hormuz: the framework hardened into something real, and that is a two-sided risk. The IRGC now says Iran and Oman have agreed each country's share of the waters and of revenues from managing the waterway — a materially more concrete step than last week's talks. But Tehran still ties reopening to lifting the US naval blockade, unfreezing assets and removing oil sanctions, and Washington says it is not talking to Iran. Brent held ~$88.3 Friday after a week down more than 5%. Watch this over the weekend — a corridor announcement while markets are shut is your single largest gap risk on Monday's open, and it cuts against you.
  • What has not changed: the calendar in front of you is still dollar-negative. Sep 1 euro-area flash HICP, Sep 4 US payrolls, Sep 9 Treasury's enlarged buybacks plus the ECB convening in Berlin, Sep 10 the ECB decision, Sep 11 US CPI. The Sept 9 hard deadline on the remaining tranche does not move.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — the technical break finally happened: DXY closed at 99.47, up 0.31% on the day and up on the week for the first time in over a month. It pushed decisively through its 200-day moving average on the Warsh headlines and ran toward the 100-DMA, recovering essentially all of the losses booked since the Treasury buyback announcement. It remains down from the 101.64 late-July high but is up ~1.4% over 12 months. 🟢 Six sessions of base-building resolved upward. The character of this tape has changed for the first time since late July.
  • Fed — hawkish chair, hawkish committee, no guidance: funds rate 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh (sworn in 22 May 2026); three regional presidents dissented for hikes in July. Warsh's Jackson Hole debut leaned hawkish on substance — inflation trends "have not meaningfully improved," the 2% target is "firm and fixed," financial conditions "not currently restrictive," the Fed will "have work to do" — while explicitly declining to signal the September move. Sept 16 hike odds ~50%, up from ~35%. Decision Sept 15–16 with the dot plot. 🟢 The most dollar-positive development on this page in a month.
  • US Treasury — the structural drag is still there, but no longer priced as dominant: the doubling of long-dated buybacks (10y–20y and 20y–30y lifted to $4bn+ per operation, effective Sept 9 through Nov 4) is what took the shine off the greenback through late August. Friday's rally recovered that entire drawdown in the index. 🔴 Still a standing negative — Washington is prioritising lower long-term yields over dollar strength, and the operations themselves have not started yet. It comes back on Sept 9.
  • ECB — hawkish repricing intact, and unspent: policy rate 2.40%, deposit rate 2.25%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin. A September hike is nearly fully priced; markets hold 2.80% by March and roughly a 60% chance of eventually reaching 3% after the French (2.7%) and Spanish (4.5%) August prints. The euro still fell on Friday. 🔴 That is the warning: the euro's hawkish support did not get to express itself against a strong dollar, so it is still there to be spent on Sept 1 and Sept 10. Euro-area July HICP 2.9%; unemployment 6.3%.
  • Strait of Hormuz — day 181, the framework is now specific: the IRGC says Iran and Oman have agreed on each country's share of the waters and of any revenues from managing the corridor; Qatar's PM added a third mediator track in Tehran on Aug 27; Oman's foreign minister is "hopeful" of formalising the arrangement soon. Tehran's conditions — lifting the naval blockade, unfreezing assets, removing oil sanctions — remain unmet, and the US says it is not currently talking to Iran. 🔴 This has moved from talks to terms. The reopening is closer than it was a week ago, and a reopening is a euro-positive terms-of-trade shock. This is now the main unhedged risk to the conversion.
  • Oil — a week of losses, then a floor: Brent ~$88.3 (roughly flat on the day) after a week down more than 5% on improving flow expectations; WTI in the low $83s. 🔴 The direction of travel is lower and every dollar off Brent is a euro-area windfall. A break into the $70s on a genuine reopening remains the fastest route back through your €0.8540 floor.
  • Rate differential — the dollar's argument, now partly being used: Fed funds 3.75% vs ECB 2.40% is a 135bp policy gap; US 10Y around 4.69% against Bunds at 3.27% is a ~142bp spread. US CPI 3.4% vs euro-area 2.9%. 🟢 Friday was the first session since late July in which carry, rather than flow and fiscal positioning, set the price. If that persists it is worth considerably more than 0.38%.

5. Two-Week Outlook

Base case: roughly €0.855 – €0.869 per dollar over the next two weeks — the whole band shifted up about 0.3% on Friday's repricing. Today's €0.8614 sits just above the midpoint, at the best level in ten days. The composition of risk has flipped: the dollar leg improved materially (hike odds 35% → 50%, DXY through its 200-DMA, benchmark revision harmless), while the euro leg's hawkish bid went unspent and Hormuz moved from talks to agreed terms. Translation — you got the good news already; the scheduled news from here leans the other way. ING's forecasts of 1.17 end-September and 1.18 end-year still imply €0.847–€0.855, i.e. below today's spot, which remains the most sobering number on this page. This is a range-hold case in which you are now near the top of the range rather than the bottom.
