Unfavourable and getting more so.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — a two-week high, powered by yields: DXY at 99.79–99.86, up ~0.2% and at its best level in two weeks. It sits just above the 38.2% Fibonacci retracement at 99.81 and comfortably above the 20-day EMA at 99.57; RSI has recovered to 52.5. Resistance stacks at 100.19 (50% retracement) and 100.57, with the swing high near 101.81. 🟢 The index has finally cleared the 99.7–99.8 shelf that rejected it four times in August.
  • Fed — hawkish chair, hawkish governors, hardening odds: funds rate 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh. Governor Michael Barr says further increases "may be warranted if inflation fails to moderate." CME FedWatch has Sept 16 at 67% (vs ~36% pre-Jackson Hole); OCBC reports a 25bp hike fully priced by October; Barclays forecasts September and December. Decision Sept 15–16 with the dot plot. 🟢 Still the most dollar-positive item on this page.
  • US labour market — the soft spot under the whole trade: ADP private payrolls +38k in August against 47k expected, the weakest since January; July revised up to 46k. Headline NFP was −23k in July with unemployment at 4.1%. Base pay growth cooled to 3.2%. 🔴 If Friday confirms a stalling labour market, the hawkish repricing that is holding your rate up becomes very hard to sustain into the FOMC.
  • ECB — September hike locked, and a second one now ~80% priced: policy rate 2.40%, deposit rate 2.25%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin (Governing Council Sept 9–10, hosted by the Bundesbank; announcement 14:15 CET). A Reuters poll has 57 of 69 economists expecting 25bp to 2.50%; markets price 2.70% by December and euro-area rates approaching 3% by late 2027. Rehn warns a prolonged conflict keeps inflation elevated; Kocher says rising risks could require a swift hike. 🔴 The standing negative, and it strengthened this week.
  • Euro-area inflation — headline at a three-year high, core cooling: August HICP 3.3% YoY, the highest since September 2023, driven almost entirely by an energy surge tied to the Middle East. Services and core inflation eased. Unemployment came in at 6.4%, above forecast, and manufacturing PMI hit a four-year high. 🟡 Mixed: the hot headline arms the hawks, but the soft core and a weaker labour print give the doves a case for one-and-done on Sep 10.
  • Strait of Hormuz — day 185, sharply escalated: US forces struck IRGC targets in southern Iran on Sep 1 (Bandar Abbas, Konarak, Chabahar, Qeshm, Sirik) after attempted attacks on commercial shipping and on US personnel. The IRGC has vowed retaliation; Trump has threatened a heavier strike. The Aug 26 Iran–Oman interim two-lane arrangement is effectively dead. 🟢 Favourable to you through the haven bid and the euro-area terms-of-trade hit — and the most reversible line on the page.
  • Oil — $95 Brent and a diesel market still screaming: Brent $95.44 (+0.84%), the highest since July 24; WTI $90.68. The US diesel crack spread has printed above $100/bbl for the first time on record. 🟡 Two-sided for you: it hurts euro-area terms of trade but also fuels the HICP prints that keep the ECB hawkish. Separately, it is a direct cost line for Impel Union's transport and delivery spend.
  • Rate differential — widening again: Fed funds 3.75% vs ECB 2.40% is a 135bp policy gap; US 10Y at 4.81% against Bunds at 3.38% is a ~144bp spread, up from ~142bp yesterday. US CPI 3.4% vs euro-area 3.3% — a gap that has essentially closed, which is the mechanism by which the ECB story eats into your rate over time. 🟢 Carry still favours the dollar; the inflation differential no longer does.

5. Two-Week Outlook

Base case: roughly €0.858 – €0.872 per dollar over the next two weeks. Today's €0.8644 sits in the upper half of it. The composition of your rate has changed and it is worth being blunt about: five days ago it was a Fed story, today it is a war story. The move from €0.8581 to €0.8644 came from strikes on Iran, $95 Brent and a 4.81% US 10-year — not from US economic strength. This morning's ADP miss (+38k vs 47k) is direct evidence of that, and it is the reason Friday's payrolls now carries more downside than upside for you. Meanwhile the ECB leg has hardened: a second hike after September is now ~80% priced versus ~60% last week. ING's forecasts of 1.17 end-September and 1.18 end-year imply €0.847–€0.855, below today's spot. Trading Economics' model sits at 1.16 this quarter (€0.862) and 1.18 in twelve months (€0.847). Every credible forecast on this page still has you converting at a worse rate later than you can today.
