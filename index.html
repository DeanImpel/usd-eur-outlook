3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — base-building, still structurally soft: DXY sits at 99.14, back above the 99.00 handle after a three-month low of 98.55 on Aug 22 and a 98.50–99.00 grind all week. It is still below its 50-EMA near 99.15 and 100-EMA near 99.48 and has weakened ~2.3% over the past month from the 101.64 late-July high. 🟡 Four sessions of stabilisation with a firm PCE behind it is the best the dollar has managed since July — and it has produced 0.4% of recovery. Tomorrow decides whether it is a base or a pause.
  • US Treasury — the structural drag is still the story: the decision to at least double buybacks of longer-dated debt (10y–20y and 20y–30y lifted to $4bn+ per operation, effective Sept 9 through Nov 4) is what took the shine off the greenback, not the data. US 10Y at 4.66%. Today's $44bn 7-year auction is the live test. 🔴 Washington is explicitly prioritising lower long-term yields over dollar strength, and that has not changed.
  • Fed — hawkish committee, untested chair, tomorrow is the event: funds rate 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh, sworn in 22 May 2026; three regional presidents dissented for hikes in July. Decision Sept 15–16 with the dot plot. The Jackson Hole keynote Friday Aug 28 at 15:00 BST is his debut in the role, landing in the same minute as the BLS payrolls benchmark revision. 🟢 Your single best scheduled chance at a better rate inside this window — and, per Goldman, the widest error bar on the page.
  • ECB — September hike priced, but the tail is being cut: policy rate 2.40%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin (Governing Council Sept 9–10, Bundesbank-hosted). A 25bp hike to a 2.5% deposit rate is nearly fully priced, but Reuters reports the Council is reluctant to signal anything beyond it, and markets have trimmed year-end expectations to under 40bp. Schnabel countered that rates may need to rise further if the Middle East conflict persists. July HICP 2.9%; euro-area unemployment 6.3%; composite PMI at a nine-month high. 🟡 Still the expensive half of your trade, but for the first time the hawkish premium is being priced out rather than in.
  • Strait of Hormuz — day 179, talks resumed, direction unchanged: Iran confirmed it has resumed discussions with Oman on managing the strait, following Tuesday's interim framework for a temporary joint maritime corridor and mine clearance. Roughly a fifth of world oil and LNG previously transited the strait. 🔴 This is the de-escalation path and on the terms-of-trade channel it is euro-positive and therefore unfavourable for you. The offsetting argument — cheaper energy undercutting the ECB's hawkish case — is now visibly showing up in rate pricing, which softens the blow but does not reverse it.
  • Oil — the slide continues: Brent ~$87.0 (−0.95%), WTI ~$81.3 (−1.15%), EU gas €65.6. Brent is down roughly $2.50 since Tuesday and about 8% off its August highs. 🟡 A move into the $70s on a genuine reopening is the fastest way for €0.8540 to stop being a floor — but it is also the fastest way to kill the ECB's September follow-through, which cuts back toward you.
  • Rate differential — still the dollar's unused argument: Fed funds 3.75% vs ECB 2.40% is a 135bp policy gap; US 10Y 4.66% against Bunds at 3.23% is a ~143bp spread. US CPI 3.4% vs euro-area 2.9%. 🟢 On carry alone the dollar is cheap here. Flow, fiscal credibility and policy signalling — not rate differentials — have run this tape since late July.

5. Two-Week Outlook

Base case: roughly €0.851 – €0.866 per dollar over the next two weeks. Today's €0.8579 sits mid-band. The band is unchanged in width, but the skew has improved slightly from yesterday: markets trimming ECB pricing to under 40bp by year-end is the first two-sided element the Sept 10 decision has had. Set against that, Hormuz de-escalation continues to leak euro-positive. ING's forecasts of 1.17 end-September and 1.18 end-year imply €0.847–€0.855 — at or below today's spot. This remains a range-hold case with one large upside trigger (Warsh, tomorrow), one new upside trigger (a dovish ECB hike on Sept 10), and two credible downside triggers (a tailing 7-year auction and a working Hormuz corridor).

This is not financial advice. FX markets are volatile and short-term moves are inherently unpredictable — treat the ranges above as scenario framing, not a guarantee. APEX Take — Conversion Strategy

€ per $1 is €0.8579, down 0.04% on the day but up 0.18% on the week — a fourth session holding above the six-month low, still 0.73% below the March average. Do not convert today. The plan does not change today, and tomorrow at 15:00 BST is the only thing on this page that can move your rate by more than a rounding error.

Hold the staged position: 40% converted, 60% held, hard deadline Sept 9. If you executed the 40% tranche, do nothing. If you have converted nothing yet, convert 40% now for near-term obligations and hold the balance. The remaining 60% converts by Sept 9 regardless of level — ahead of the Sept 10 ECB decision, the Sept 15–16 FOMC, and the start of Treasury's enlarged buybacks on Sept 9. That is a dated rule, not a forecast, and it is what keeps this from becoming an open-ended bet.

The tactical trigger is live and tomorrow is when it can fire: if the rate trades above €0.863 at any point during or after Warsh's keynote, convert the remaining 60% into that strength rather than waiting for Sept 9. That is the top of the base band and above what ING's own forecasts say you should expect this quarter. Set the €0.863 rate alert with your bank today, before the close. The keynote and the payrolls benchmark revision land in the same minute — you will not have time to react manually, and Goldman is warning clients about exactly this volatility window.

What changed today, and it is mildly in your favour: money markets cut year-end ECB tightening expectations to under 40bp after Reuters reported the Council is reluctant to signal anything past September. That makes the Sept 10 decision genuinely two-sided for the first time — a hike delivered with a pause signal is a sell-the-fact for the euro. It does not justify holding past Sept 9. It justifies not panicking if the rate drifts lower between now and then.

Standing recommendation for recurring transfers: the honest scoreboard is that timing has cost you roughly 2.6% against the July 28 peak. Ask your bank's FX desk what a one-month forward on your typical conversion size costs. For a recurring, known-amount paycheck conversion, a forward removes this entire problem and is a cleaner instrument than any staging rule I can write for you.
