1. Bottom Line

🟢 The euro's biggest scheduled catalyst of the week fired this morning — and your rate went up, not down. Every dollar buys €0.8625 — +0.10% on the day and +0.69% on the week. EUR/USD is 1.1594, holding below 1.1600 after euro-area flash HICP came in at 3.3% (from 2.9%), exactly as forecast, while core inflation edged down to 2.4% from 2.5%. That split is the whole story: the headline jump is energy pass-through from $90 Brent, not underlying price pressure, so the print delivered no fresh hawkish fuel beyond what was already priced. This is a good window and you should use it. The catalyst that could have taken back last week's gain has now passed harmlessly — but the ECB still hikes on Sep 10, US payrolls land Friday, and the calendar from here gets more dangerous, not less. Convert the remaining balance today.
  • Six months ago (March) $1 bought roughly €0.8642 on average. Today $1 gets you €0.8625 — a 0.20% half-year loss, the narrowest that gap has been since July. The €0.8807 peak of Jul 28 is 2.07% above you; the €0.8540 floor of Aug 21 is 1.00% below. You are in the upper half of the six-month range and have been for four straight sessions.
  • Today's HICP was the test, and the euro failed to rally on it. Headline euro-area inflation jumped to 3.3% from 2.9% — a large move — yet EUR/USD did not break higher, because it landed exactly on consensus and because core fell to 2.4%. The market read it as an oil shock passing through, not as evidence the ECB needs to go beyond the hike it has already telegraphed. Favourable for you — the euro's hawkish ammunition was spent without moving your rate.
  • The energy channel is now working on both sides of the ledger. Brent sits near $90 with Société Générale calling for an uptrend toward $102 and $110; the ECB's own modelling puts every sustained $10 on oil at roughly +0.5pp on euro-area HICP. Higher energy hurts the euro area's terms of trade (good for you) but also feeds the inflation prints that justify ECB tightening (bad for you). Today those two forces cancelled. Do not assume they keep cancelling.
  • US–Iran tensions escalated again overnight and the dollar is the safe haven. Following Sunday's US strikes on Iranian rocket launchers preparing to mine the Strait of Hormuz, risk aversion has bid the dollar, knocked gold below $4,400, and held DXY near 99.50 despite Monday's 0.3% slip. Favourable — but this is the most reversible line on the page, and it has flipped four times since March.
  • The Fed leg is still the strongest thing you have. Warsh's Jackson Hole debut — inflation has not meaningfully slowed, the 2% target "firm and fixed," the Fed still has "work to do" — has Sept 16 hike odds near 50%, up from ~35% pre-speech. OCBC frames Fed restraint as the reason the dollar is grinding higher against peers. Global yields are rising with it: Japan's 10-year hit 3% for the first time since 1996. Favourable.
  • The one political wildcard: Trump is publicly leaning on the Fed again. He said rates are "too high," while adding he has "a lot of respect" for Warsh and that Warsh "will know what he has to do." Sustained pressure on a new chair is dollar-negative if markets start discounting Fed independence. Watch it, do not trade it.
  • The calendar in front of you is dollar-negative on balance. Sep 4 US payrolls, Sep 9 Treasury's enlarged buybacks begin and the ECB convenes in Berlin, Sep 10 the ECB decision, Sep 11 US CPI, Sep 15–16 FOMC. The Sep 9 hard deadline on the remaining tranche does not move.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — grinding, not surging: DXY around 99.50, clinging to marginal gains after slipping ~0.3% on Monday. The index remains above its 200-day moving average after clearing it on Friday, but has repeatedly failed at the 100-day SMA / 38.2% Fibonacci confluence near 99.7–99.8. It sits below the 101.4–101.6 late-July high and roughly +1.3% over 12 months. 🟢 Constructive consolidation above a broken level — still not a trend.
  • Fed — hawkish chair, hawkish committee, no guidance: funds rate 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh (sworn in 22 May 2026); three regional presidents dissented for hikes in July. Warsh leaned hawkish on substance at Jackson Hole while declining to signal September and calling for a "quieter" central bank. Sept 16 hike odds ~50%, up from ~35%. Decision Sept 15–16 with the dot plot. 🟢 Still the most dollar-positive item on this page.
  • ECB — the September hike is now effectively locked, and that is already in the price: policy rate 2.40%, deposit rate 2.25%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin (Governing Council meets Sept 9–10, hosted by the Bundesbank; announcement 14:15 CET). Executive board member Isabel Schnabel has made an explicit case for another increase, and today's German CPI at 2.9% plus euro-area headline at 3.3% make a hike close to certain. Markets hold 2.80% by March and roughly a 60% chance of eventually reaching 3%. 🔴 A standing negative — but note it did not move your rate today, which tells you how much of it is already discounted.
  • Euro-area inflation — headline hot, core cooling: flash August HICP 3.3% YoY (from 2.9%, matching consensus) while core edged down to 2.4% from 2.5%. 🟢 The core softening is the single most euro-negative data point of the last two weeks and it arrived today. It is the reason the euro could not rally on a 40bp headline jump, and it gives the doves on the Governing Council something to hold on Sep 10.
  • US Treasury — the structural drag arrives Sept 9: the doubling of long-dated buybacks (10y–20y and 20y–30y lifted to $4bn+ per operation, effective Sept 9 through Nov 4) is what took the shine off the greenback through late August. 🔴 A scheduled negative that has not started yet — Washington is prioritising lower long-term yields over dollar strength.
  • Strait of Hormuz — day 184, escalating again: US Central Command's strikes on Aug 30 against Iranian rocket launchers preparing to mine the waterway have undercut the 60-day interim two-lane route Iran and Oman agreed on Aug 26. Risk aversion is bidding the dollar and pressuring gold. Roughly 6–8 mb/d still transits without a US–Iran peace agreement. 🟢 Favourable to you today — and the most reversible item on the page.
  • Oil — the $90 handle is holding and the street is looking higher: Brent near $90 after Monday's ~3% jump, with Société Générale flagging an uptrend targeting $102 and $110. The US diesel crack spread has printed above $100/bbl for the first time on record. 🟡 Genuinely two-sided for you: a euro-area terms-of-trade hit, but also the fuel for the HICP prints that keep the ECB hawkish. Meanwhile it is a direct cost line for Impel Union's transport and delivery spend.
  • Rate differential — the dollar's structural argument, compressing at the margin: Fed funds 3.75% vs ECB 2.40% is a 135bp policy gap; US 10Y around 4.72% against Bunds at 3.30% is a ~142bp spread. Global yields are rising broadly on energy, fiscal risk premia and tightening expectations — Japan's 10-year cleared 3% for the first time since 1996. US CPI 3.4% vs euro-area headline 3.3% — a gap that has now essentially closed. 🟢 Carry still favours the dollar, but the inflation differential no longer does, and that is the mechanism by which the ECB story eats your rate.
