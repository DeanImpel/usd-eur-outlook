3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — a one-week high into the keynote: DXY at 99.22, up 0.06% on the day and set for its first weekly gain in over a month, having recovered from the 98.55 three-month low of Aug 22. It remains down ~2.3% over the past month from the 101.64 late-July high but is up 1.4% over 12 months. 🟢 Five sessions of base-building, and the market chose to buy dollars ahead of Warsh rather than sell them. That is a positioning tell in your favour.
  • Fed — hawkish committee, untested chair, speaking now: funds rate 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh (sworn in 22 May 2026); three regional presidents dissented for hikes in July. Decision Sept 15–16 with the dot plot. The Jackson Hole keynote at 10:00 ET today is his debut, alongside the BLS payrolls benchmark revision. 🟢 Your single largest scheduled chance at a better rate inside this window — and the widest error bar on the page. The specific question: does he lean against the easing in financial conditions that Bessent's buyback intervention created?
  • US Treasury — the structural drag remains the story: the doubling of long-dated buybacks (10y–20y and 20y–30y lifted to $4bn+ per operation, effective Sept 9 through Nov 4) is what took the shine off the greenback, not the data. US 10Y at 4.69%. 🔴 Washington is explicitly prioritising lower long-term yields over dollar strength, and nothing this week has changed that.
  • ECB — hawkish repricing is back on: policy rate 2.40%, deposit rate 2.25%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin. A September hike is nearly fully priced, and after yesterday's French (2.7%) and Spanish (4.5%) inflation prints markets now price 2.80% by March and ~2.90% by late 2027 — about a 60% chance of eventually reaching 3%. The ECB minutes pointed to another likely increase; Reuters says policymakers are prepared to hike in September to contain the fallout from the Iran war but are reluctant to signal beyond that. Euro-area July HICP 2.9%; unemployment 6.3%; economic sentiment at a seven-month high. 🔴 This is the expensive half of your trade and it re-tightened overnight.
  • Strait of Hormuz — day 180, three mediators in a week, Iran raising the price: Qatar's PM held talks in Tehran yesterday; an Iran–Oman phased framework for a temporary corridor and mine clearance is under technical negotiation. But Iran is now compiling formal conditions — lifting the naval blockade, unfreezing assets abroad, removing oil sanctions — and Washington says it is not currently talking to Tehran. 🟢 For the first time in a week this reads as a slower de-escalation, which supports oil and undercuts the euro-area terms-of-trade windfall. Favourable at the margin.
  • Oil — the slide has stalled: Brent ~$88.1, WTI ~$83.1, EU gas €69.6 (+1.9%). Brent is roughly $1 above Wednesday's level after five sessions of decline. 🟢 Firmer energy is a euro-area cost, not a windfall — mildly more euro per dollar. A break back into the $70s on a genuine reopening remains the fastest route through your €0.8540 floor.
  • Rate differential — still the dollar's unused argument: Fed funds 3.75% vs ECB 2.40% is a 135bp policy gap; US 10Y 4.69% against Bunds at 3.27% is a ~142bp spread. US CPI 3.4% vs euro-area 2.9%. 🟢 On carry alone the dollar is cheap here. Flow, fiscal credibility and policy signalling — not rate differentials — have run this tape since late July, and that is exactly what Warsh can address today.

5. Two-Week Outlook

Base case: roughly €0.852 – €0.866 per dollar over the next two weeks. Today's €0.8589 sits mid-band, slightly above the midpoint for the first time this week. The width is unchanged but the composition of the risk has shifted: the ECB leg has re-tightened after the French and Spanish inflation prints (against you), while Hormuz has slowed and oil has firmed (for you). Those roughly offset, leaving today's keynote as the dominant variable. ING's forecasts of 1.17 end-September and 1.18 end-year still imply €0.847–€0.855 — at or below today's spot, which is the single most sobering number on this page. This remains a range-hold case with one large live upside trigger (Warsh, in the next two hours) and two credible downside triggers (an upside euro-area HICP surprise on Sept 1 and a hawkish ECB on Sept 10).
