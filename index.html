1. Bottom Line

🔴 Every dollar you convert today buys ~€0.865 — down 0.3% on the day and 0.8% on the week, the weakest reading since mid-June. The July jobs report was the break: payrolls contracted 23,000 against +80K expected, with 103K stripped out of the prior two months, and September Fed hike odds fell from 57% to 44% within minutes. This is a poor window and the one thing propping it up — a rebound in oil after Iran published restrictive Hormuz transit terms — is not strong enough to offset a labor market that is now shrinking.
  • Six months ago (March) $1 bought roughly €0.8642 on average. Today $1 gets you €0.8651 — essentially flat over half a year, and 1.8% below the €0.8807 six-month high set on Jul 28.
  • The dollar broke, it did not drift. The Dollar Index closed near 99.61, −0.32% on the day (intraday low 99.4, a two-month low) and −1.37% over the past month. EUR/USD trades near 1.1560, back at a seven-week high.
  • The Fed leg is now broken, not bending. July payrolls printed −23K vs +80K consensus — below every estimate in the survey — with May and June revised down a combined 103K, dropping the three-month average to just 20K. Unemployment fell to 4.1%, but only because roughly a million workers have left the labor force since May. Average hourly earnings growth slipped to 3.2%, the lowest since 2021. September hike odds: 44% (CME FedWatch), from 57% pre-data and ~81% on Aug 3.
  • Hormuz went the other way this week — and it did not save you. Iran published a restrictive draft plan on Aug 6 (US and Israeli ships banned, compensation demanded from other states, 20%-of-cargo penalties), sending Brent up 3.8% to $82.49 and WTI to $77.29. Iran and Oman have agreed a proposed shipping corridor but no US–Iran deal has been finalized. Oil back above $82 would normally rebuild the Fed hike case; the payrolls collapse overwhelmed it.
  • The euro side is steady rather than surging. Q2 euro-area GDP +0.4%, July HICP 2.9% YoY, unemployment 6.3%, and stronger-than-expected German June factory orders. Markets fully price one more ECB hike by year-end with roughly a 40% chance of a second — softer than a week ago, but the differential is still compressing against you because the US side is falling faster.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — at a two-month low: DXY ~99.61, −0.32% on the day with an intraday low of 99.4, and −1.37% on the month. The index has spent the week below 100 and every attempted bounce has been sold. 🔴 Unfavorable — fewer euro per dollar than at any point since early June.
  • Fed — September hike odds down to 44% after a payrolls contraction: The July 29 decision was a 9–3 hold at 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh, with three dissenters wanting an immediate quarter-point hike. Today's −23K print, plus 103K of downward revisions, removes the labor-market cover for tightening. Odds of a hike by October sit at ~58%; the odds the Fed simply holds in September are now ~60%, versus one-in-three a week ago. Next decision Sept 15–16, with the dot plot. 🔴 Your largest active drag.
  • Strait of Hormuz — no deal, and Iran's terms hardened: Iran and Oman agreed a proposed shipping corridor, but Iran's published draft would ban US and Israeli vessels, require compensation from states that "harmed Iran," and levy 20%-of-cargo penalties. Trump says talks are "moving along"; nothing is signed. Brent ~$82–83, WTI ~$77–78 after a 3.8% jump on Aug 6. 🟡 Mixed for you — higher oil supports the US inflation impulse (helpful), but a genuine reopening headline remains the single largest downside risk.
  • ECB — hiking bias intact but softening at the margin: Deposit rate 2.40%, unchanged at the last meeting. Markets fully price one more hike by year-end with ~40% odds of a second, down from near-full pricing of a September move a week ago as cheaper energy fed through. Next decision Sept 10. 🔴 Still a euro-supportive stance, just a less aggressive one.
  • Rate differential — compressing from the US side now: Through July the story was oil pushing the Fed hawkish. Today it is the US labor market pulling the Fed dovish while the euro area posts 0.4% growth and 2.9% inflation. 🔴 The gap that pays you euro per dollar is narrowing regardless of what oil does next.

5. Two-Week Outlook

Base case: expect roughly €0.855 – €0.872 per dollar over the next two weeks. Today's €0.8651 sits mid-band, but the skew is now clearly negative — a contracting labor market is a slower-moving, harder-to-reverse driver than an oil headline, and the next scheduled chance to reverse it is the Aug 12 CPI. Beyond that there is nothing structural until the Sept 10 ECB and Sept 15–16 FOMC.

APEX Take — Conversion Strategy

€ per $1 fell to €0.8651, down 0.3% on the day and 0.8% on the week. Today changed the character of the trade. Through July you were being hurt by a headline risk — a Hormuz deal that might or might not land. As of this morning you are being hurt by a data trend: US payrolls contracted 23,000 with 103K stripped from prior months, and September Fed hike odds went 57% → 44% inside an hour. Note the asymmetry that did not help you: oil rebounded above $82 on Iran's restrictive draft terms, which should have supported the dollar, and the dollar still made a two-month low. When the supportive catalyst fires and the rate falls anyway, that is information.

Recommendation: Move to 80% converted now (up from 70% on Aug 5). The remaining 20% is a single-event bet on the Aug 12 CPI — the only scheduled release in this window that can rebuild the hike case. Place a limit order at €0.870 with your bank's FX desk to catch a bounce if oil keeps climbing on stalled Hormuz talks. If CPI comes in at or below June's 3.5%, convert the balance the same day; the market will start pricing cuts, not hikes, and there will be no second look. If CPI runs hot and Sept odds recover above 60%, hold the final 20% into the Sept 15–16 FOMC.
