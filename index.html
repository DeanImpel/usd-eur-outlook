1. Bottom Line

🟡 Every dollar you convert today buys ~€0.869 — essentially flat on the day (+0.1%) but still about 1.4% below where it stood a week ago, with the euro holding just above the $1.15 handle near its best level since mid-June. This remains a poor near-term window and today made it worse in substance if not in price: US–Iran talks on reopening the Strait of Hormuz resumed today, oil fell hard, and September Fed hike odds collapsed from ~81% to ~65% in a single session — the pillar that was holding € per $1 up is eroding faster than the spot rate shows.
  • Six months ago (March) $1 bought roughly €0.8642 on average. Today $1 gets you €0.8688 — a net gain of about half a percent over half a year, and still 1.4% below the €0.8807 six-month high set on Jul 28.
  • The dollar stabilized today but the weekly picture is ugly. The Dollar Index sits at ~99.97, roughly flat on the day after gapping to an intraday low near 99.29 on Monday, and down about 1.5% on the week from late-July highs near 101.50 — its worst weekly performance in three months. EUR/USD trades near 1.1510.
  • The Fed leg is breaking. Market pricing for a September 15–16 hike has fallen to roughly 65%, down from ~81% on Aug 3 and ~77% before the July 29 meeting. That repricing is a direct consequence of the oil collapse — the inflation impulse that justified the hike is being priced out. This is the single most important negative development for you today.
  • Hormuz: talks are live, not theoretical. US–Iran bilateral talks on reopening the Strait resumed today, Aug 4, after Trump cancelled the planned strike on Aug 2. The working framework calls for full reopening with no Iranian transit tolls for at least 60 days. Brent fell about 5% to ~$83.51 and WTI about 5.7% to ~$79.87 on the news. Unresolved: control of the strait and who collects transit fees.
  • The euro's own bid is strengthening. Q2 euro-area GDP grew 0.4% (double consensus, fastest since early 2025) and July HICP firmed to 2.9% YoY with core and services both accelerating. Markets now price 70–79% odds of an ECB hike to 2.50% on Sept 10, and a deposit rate of 2.75% by early 2027. Rate differential is compressing from both ends — against you.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — stabilizing at a low level after a bad week: DXY at ~99.97, roughly flat today, but down ~1.5% on the week and off ~1.5 points from the late-July peak near 101.50. Monday's gap down to 99.29 followed confirmed coordinated US–Japan yen-buying intervention, which is dollar-negative across the board. 🔴 Unfavorable for you — fewer euro per dollar than a week ago.
  • Fed — September hike odds down to ~65% from ~81% in a day: The July 29 decision was a 9–3 hold at 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh, with three dissenters wanting an immediate ¼-point hike. Warsh has since signalled reluctance to commit to hiking in response to energy-driven inflation. With oil falling, the hike case is unwinding. 🔴 Newly unfavorable — this flipped from your strongest support to a live negative.
  • Strait of Hormuz — talks resumed today, oil down ~5%: US–Iran bilateral negotiations on reopening restarted Aug 4, framework is full reopening with no tolls for 60 days. Brent ~$83.51, WTI ~$79.87. Sticking points remain control of the strait and transit-fee collection, so a signed deal could still take time. 🔴 Every step toward reopening strips the dollar's safe-haven premium and its inflation impulse simultaneously.
  • ECB — hiking bias hardening, Sept 10 priced at 70–79%: Q2 GDP +0.4%, July HICP 2.9% YoY with core and services firming. Markets price the deposit rate reaching 2.75% by early 2027 — two more hikes. Next decision Sept 10. 🔴 A hawkish, accelerating euro area works directly against € per $1.
  • Energy pass-through — now cutting against you on both sides: Euro-area energy inflation ran 10.0% YoY in July, so falling oil relieves Europe more than the US on growth, while simultaneously removing the US inflation impulse that supported the Fed hike trade. 🔴 The same catalyst hurts you twice.
