1. Bottom Line

🟡 Every dollar you convert today buys €0.8573 — up 0.13% on the day, the first daily gain in six sessions after Friday printed a six-month low of €0.8540, but still down 0.71% on the week. The bounce is real but thin: EUR/USD backed off Friday's 1.1710 three-month high to 1.1665 as the market squares up ahead of a genuinely loaded week — Bessent's Iran "economic D-Day" sanctions today, US core PCE Wednesday, a $44bn 7-year Treasury auction Thursday, and Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote Friday. This is a pause in a downtrend, not a turn. Holding the balance of your conversion to Friday is still the right call; converting into a 0.13% bounce is not.
  • Six months ago (March) $1 bought roughly €0.8642 on average. Today $1 gets you €0.8573 — a 0.80% half-year loss. The €0.8807 peak of Jul 28 is still 2.7% above you, and Friday's €0.8540 floor is only 0.4% below.
  • Last week's PMI beat did not stick — but it did stop the bleeding. The US flash composite at 56.0 with services at 56.8 was the strongest activity read since April 2022, and EUR/USD has now stalled rather than extended. Four straight up-days for the euro ended at the 1.1660/70 support ING flags as the line that matters. Neutral-to-favourable — the euro is no longer being handed free upside.
  • ING's house view remains dollar-negative and that is the honest read of the setup. Their EUR/USD forecasts are 1.17 for end-September and 1.18 for end-year — i.e. roughly €0.847–€0.855 per dollar, at or below today. They flag the debasement trade, US fiscal credibility questions and a re-escalating tariff war (now including Canada) as dollar negatives. Fewer euro per dollar is the base path they are underwriting.
  • The one scheduled event that cuts your way is Warsh on Friday. ING calls it explicitly: after his July press conference triggered a long-end sell-off, he "will have to double-down on the Fed's inflation-fighting credentials," making the speech a hawkish event risk for the dollar. More euro per dollar if delivered. That is why the Sept 9 deadline on your remaining tranche sits where it does.
  • The ECB leg has not got cheaper. Deposit rate 2.40%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin, with a 25bp hike still above 90% priced after July HICP at 2.9% headline / 2.5% core. Euro-area composite PMI is at a nine-month high and Chicago futures positioning shows asset managers and leveraged funds buying euro — speculators are underweight and adding. Fewer euro per dollar on both policy and flow.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — stabilising at the lows, not recovering: DXY sits at 98.96, +0.17% on the day, after setting a three-month low of 98.56 on Aug 20. The index is still roughly 2.6% below the late-July high near 101.60. ING sees consolidation in a 98.50–99.00 range today "with greater risk seen on the downside." 🟡 The single largest driver of your rate has stopped falling — that is the whole of the good news.
  • Iran sanctions — today's live wire, and the sign is ambiguous: Treasury Secretary Bessent unveils what he has billed as "an economic D-Day — the single greatest financial offensive ever marshaled against an adversary." Iran's national security chief Mohsen Rezaei has threatened to seize non-compliant vessels and vowed "not even a single drop of oil will leave the region." Oil fell into the announcement: WTI ~$85.65 (−1.6%), Brent ~$93.09 (−1.4%). 🟡 Classic safe-haven dollar bid on escalation, but ING notes that if the sanctions hit Chinese buyers of Iranian crude and reopen the tariff war, that is dollar negative on this positioning.
  • US Treasury — the structural drag, and a live test Thursday: Buyback operations in the 10y–20y and 20y–30y sectors at least double to $4bn+ per operation effective Sept 9 through Nov 4. Bessent has also dangled a fiscal consolidation plan after last week's long-end stress, though ING expects efficiency gains rather than real cuts. Thursday brings a $44bn 7-year auction. US 10Y at 4.71%. 🔴 Washington is still explicitly prioritising lower long-term yields over dollar strength.
  • Fed — hawkish committee, guarded chair, Friday is the event: Funds rate 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh; three regional presidents dissented for hikes in July. Decision Sept 15–16 with the dot plot. Jackson Hole keynote Friday Aug 28. ING: he "will have to double-down on the Fed's inflation-fighting credentials… could be a hawkish event risk for the dollar." 🟢 Your single best scheduled chance at a better rate inside this window.
  • ECB — September hike still above 90% priced: Deposit rate 2.40%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin (Governing Council meets Sept 9–10, Bundesbank-hosted). July HICP 2.9% headline / 2.5% core; euro-area unemployment 6.3%; consumer inflation expectations easing to 2.9% but still above target. EU gas €68.53/MWh (+4.0%) keeps the upside inflation channel open. 🔴 The expensive half of your trade, and it is not getting cheaper.
  • Strait of Hormuz — day 176, effectively closed, sign still ambiguous: Oil shipments through the strait have virtually halted; the US-protected corridor is moving roughly half pre-war volume and Tehran is now threatening to seize vessels transiting outside its rules. 🟡 Escalation bids the dollar via haven flow but simultaneously feeds euro-area energy inflation and ECB hawkishness — the two channels have been roughly cancelling for weeks.
  • Rate differential — still the dollar's unused argument: Fed funds 3.75% vs ECB 2.40% is a 135bp policy gap; US 10Y 4.71% against Bunds at 3.26% is a ~145bp spread. 🟢 On carry alone the dollar is cheap here. Flow, fiscal credibility and policy signalling — not rate differentials — are running this tape, and have been since late July.

5. Two-Week Outlook

Base case: roughly €0.851 – €0.865 per dollar over the next two weeks. Today's €0.8573 sits mid-band. The band has tightened slightly from Friday because the euro's four-day run stalled at the 1.1660/70 support ING flags, but the skew is still against you: ING's official forecasts are 1.17 end-September and 1.18 end-year, which implies €0.847–€0.855 — at or below today's spot. This is a range-hold case with one credible upside trigger (Warsh, Friday) and two credible downside triggers (a soft PCE, a bad 7-year auction).

More euro per dollar

€0.862 – €0.874

Core PCE runs hot Wednesday, the $44bn 7-year auction clears cleanly, and Warsh reasserts inflation-fighting credibility on Friday — ING's own "hawkish event risk for the dollar." The Iran sanctions produce haven demand without a China escalation. Sep 4 payrolls confirming the PMI hiring signal takes you to the top of this band.

Range-bound (most likely)

€0.851 – €0.865

Sanctions land without a tariff escalation, PCE lands in line, the auction is unremarkable, and Warsh stays on the symposium's payments-and-innovation theme. EUR/USD chops between 1.1570 and 1.1750, with 1.1660/70 as the pivot. You get roughly today's rate with a better week than last.

Fewer euro per dollar

€0.842 – €0.851

The sanctions catch Chinese buyers and reopen the tariff war, a cool PCE flips the market to pricing a Q4 cut, or the 7-year auction tails and revives the debasement trade. Any one path alone takes you through Friday's €0.8540 six-month low to a new floor.

This is not financial advice. FX markets are volatile and short-term moves are inherently unpredictable — treat the ranges above as scenario framing, not a guarantee. For a transaction of meaningful size, consider consulting a licensed financial advisor or your bank's treasury/FX desk, especially for hedging tools (forward contracts, limit orders) that remove timing risk entirely.
APEX Take — Conversion Strategy

€ per $1 is €0.8573, up 0.13% on the day — the first daily gain in six sessions — but still down 0.71% on the week and only 0.4% above Friday's six-month low of €0.8540. A 0.13% bounce is not a signal. Nothing about the plan changes today.

Hold the staged position: 40% converted, 60% held, hard deadline Sept 9. If you executed the 40% tranche last week, do nothing today. If you have converted nothing yet, convert 40% now for near-term obligations and hold the balance. The remaining 60% converts by Sept 9 regardless of level — ahead of the Sept 10 ECB decision, the Sept 15–16 FOMC, and the start of Treasury's enlarged buybacks on Sept 9. That is a dated rule, not a forecast, and it is what keeps this from becoming an open-ended bet.

One tactical adjustment worth making this week: if the rate trades above €0.862 at any point between Wednesday's PCE and Friday's Warsh keynote, convert the remaining 60% into that strength rather than waiting for the Sept 9 deadline. That level is the top of the base band and it is the level ING's own forecasts say you should not expect to see again this quarter. Set the alert; do not watch the screen.

Standing recommendation for recurring transfers: six sessions of this and the honest scoreboard is that timing has cost you roughly 2.7% against the July 28 peak. Ask your bank's FX desk what a one-month forward on your typical conversion size costs. For a recurring, known-amount paycheck conversion, a forward removes this entire problem and is a cleaner instrument than any staging rule I can write for you.
