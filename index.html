1. Bottom Line

🟡 Every dollar you convert today buys €0.8573 — up 0.09% on the day and now up 0.11% on the week, the third consecutive small gain off Friday's six-month low of €0.8540, but still 0.80% below the March average. EUR/USD sits at 1.1664 after failing again at the 1.1700 shelf. Today is not a conversion decision — core PCE prints at 8:30am ET and Warsh speaks Friday. Converting into a 0.09% drift hours before the Fed's preferred inflation gauge is a coin flip you are not being paid to take.
  • Six months ago (March) $1 bought roughly €0.8642 on average. Today $1 gets you €0.8573 — a 0.80% half-year loss. The €0.8807 peak of Jul 28 is still 2.7% above you; Friday's €0.8540 floor is 0.4% below.
  • The floor has held three sessions running. The euro has now failed twice at 1.1700–1.1710 and the dollar index is grinding back toward 99.00. Marginally favourable — but this is base-building, not a trend change, and it has happened on the quietest three days of the month.
  • Today's core PCE is the first real test, and it lands this morning. Consensus is +0.2% m/m core with the annual rate holding at 3.3%, alongside the second estimate of Q2 GDP and durable goods. ING's read is that a benign 0.2% print keeps the dollar flat and that 99.00/99.10 caps DXY on the topside. A hot print rebuilds the September hike case and is your cleanest realistic upside inside this window (more euro per dollar).
  • Hormuz just changed direction — and it cuts against you. Iran and Oman announced an interim framework for a temporary joint maritime corridor plus a mine-clearance project. Brent fell ~3% to about $89.5 and WTI to ~$81, roughly 6% lower over three sessions. Cheaper energy is a terms-of-trade windfall for a net energy importer: euro-supportive, fewer euro per dollar. The single largest unscheduled variable on this page has begun to resolve in the wrong direction for you.
  • Friday remains the event. Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as Chair lands at 15:00 BST — in the same minute as the BLS payrolls benchmark revision — 19 days before the Sept 16 FOMC. No track record as Chair means the widest plausible reaction range in both directions. That collision is exactly why the Sept 9 deadline on your remaining tranche sits where it does.
  • The ECB leg has not cheapened. Deposit rate 2.40%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin, a 25bp hike still above 90% priced after July HICP at 2.9%. Falling energy is the one thing that could soften that pricing — but not before the Sept 1 flash HICP. Fewer euro per dollar on policy for now.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — stabilising, still structurally soft: DXY sits near 98.98, up 0.1% on the day, holding just below the 99.00 handle after a three-month low of 98.55 on Aug 22. The index remains below its 50-EMA at 99.15 and 100-EMA at 99.48 — the short-term trend is still bearish — and it has weakened 2.5% over the past month. 🟡 Three sessions of stabilisation is exactly what a base looks like and exactly what a pause before the next leg down looks like. Today's data decides which.
  • US Treasury — the structural drag remains the story: the decision to at least double buybacks of longer-dated debt (10y–20y and 20y–30y lifted to $4bn+ per operation, effective Sept 9 through Nov 4) is what took the shine off the greenback, not the data. US 10Y at 4.70%. Thursday brings a $44bn 7-year auction as the live test. 🔴 Washington is explicitly prioritising lower long-term yields over dollar strength.
  • Fed — hawkish committee, untested chair, Friday is the event: funds rate 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh, sworn in 22 May 2026; three regional presidents dissented for hikes in July. Decision Sept 15–16 with the dot plot. The Jackson Hole keynote on Friday Aug 28 is his debut in the role, landing in the same minute as the BLS payrolls benchmark revision. 🟢 Your single best scheduled chance at a better rate inside this window — and the widest error bar on the page.
  • ECB — September hike still above 90% priced: deposit rate 2.40%, decision Sept 10 in Berlin (Governing Council Sept 9–10, Bundesbank-hosted). July HICP 2.9% headline; euro-area unemployment 6.3%; composite PMI at a nine-month high with manufacturing the strongest in four years. 🔴 The expensive half of your trade. The energy collapse is the first genuine argument for softer euro-area inflation in months, but it will not show up in the data before Sept 1.
  • Strait of Hormuz — day 178, and the direction just flipped: Iranian FM Araghchi and Oman's Badr Albusaidi announced a joint "interim framework" to reopen shipping via a temporary joint maritime corridor, paired with mine clearance; technical talks continue toward a permanent corridor, traffic-management mechanism and security services. Roughly a fifth of world oil and LNG previously transited the strait. 🔴 This is the de-escalation path, and on the terms-of-trade channel it is euro-positive and therefore unfavourable for you. Note the ambiguity has not vanished: cheaper energy also undercuts the ECB's hawkish case, which cuts the other way into Sept 10.
  • Oil — the news is being sold hard: Brent ~$89.5 (−3%), WTI ~$81, down roughly 6% across three sessions. The Iran sanctions package earlier this week failed to produce a bid; the Oman corridor headline produced a sell-off. 🟡 A move toward the $70s on a genuine reopening would be a clean euro-positive shock — the fastest way for €0.8540 to stop being a floor.
  • Rate differential — still the dollar's unused argument: Fed funds 3.75% vs ECB 2.40% is a 135bp policy gap; US 10Y 4.70% against Bunds at 3.25% is a ~145bp spread. 🟢 On carry alone the dollar is cheap here. Flow, fiscal credibility and policy signalling — not rate differentials — have run this tape since late July.

5. Two-Week Outlook

Base case: roughly €0.851 – €0.865 per dollar over the next two weeks. Today's €0.8573 sits mid-band. The band is unchanged in width but the skew has worsened slightly: the Hormuz de-escalation adds a euro-positive channel that was not live yesterday. ING's official forecasts of 1.17 end-September and 1.18 end-year imply €0.847–€0.855 — at or below today's spot. This remains a range-hold case with one credible upside trigger (Warsh, Friday) and three credible downside triggers (a soft PCE today, a tailing 7-year auction Thursday, and a working Hormuz corridor).

APEX Take — Conversion Strategy

€ per $1 is €0.8573, up 0.09% on the day and up 0.11% on the week — a third consecutive small gain, but still 0.80% below the March average and only 0.4% above the six-month low. Three quiet up-days into the two events that actually matter is not a signal. Nothing about the plan changes today.

Hold the staged position: 40% converted, 60% held, hard deadline Sept 9. If you executed the 40% tranche, do nothing today. If you have converted nothing yet, convert 40% now for near-term obligations and hold the balance. The remaining 60% converts by Sept 9 regardless of level — ahead of the Sept 10 ECB decision, the Sept 15–16 FOMC, and the start of Treasury's enlarged buybacks on Sept 9. That is a dated rule, not a forecast, and it is what keeps this from becoming an open-ended bet.

The tactical trigger stands, and this is the week it can fire: if the rate trades above €0.863 at any point between this morning's PCE and Friday's Warsh keynote, convert the remaining 60% into that strength rather than waiting for Sept 9. That is the top of the base band and the level ING's own forecasts say you should not expect to see again this quarter. Set a rate alert at €0.863 today. Friday's keynote and the payrolls benchmark revision land in the same minute — you will not have time to react manually.

One thing genuinely changed today: the Iran–Oman corridor framework moves Hormuz from an ambiguous two-sided risk to a directional one that works against you. A functioning corridor means cheaper energy, a terms-of-trade windfall for the euro area, and fewer euro per dollar. That argues for treating Sept 9 as a ceiling on patience, not a target — if the €0.863 trigger fires, take it and stop managing this.

Standing recommendation for recurring transfers: the honest scoreboard is that timing has cost you roughly 2.7% against the July 28 peak. Ask your bank's FX desk what a one-month forward on your typical conversion size costs. For a recurring, known-amount paycheck conversion, a forward removes this entire problem and is a cleaner instrument than any staging rule I can write for you.
