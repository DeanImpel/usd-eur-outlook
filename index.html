1. Bottom Line

🟡 Every dollar you convert today buys ~€0.867 — a shade below Thursday, 0.3% better than a week ago, and effectively unchanged over six months. The level is stable; the foundation under it is not. Two cool inflation prints in two days took September hike odds from 55% to 32%, and Brent broke −2.3% to $86.91 as Iran and Oman agreed a Hormuz framework. Both supports for your number weakened this week while the number itself barely moved — that gap is what closes against you next.
  • Six months ago (March) $1 bought roughly €0.8642 on average. Today $1 gets you €0.8669 — a rounding error over half a year, and 1.6% below the €0.8807 six-month high set on Jul 28.
  • The week reads better than it deserves to. € per $1 is up 0.3% from Aug 7 only because Aug 7 was the euro's spike high on the payrolls shock (EUR/USD 1.157). Measured from the July 28 peak you are down 1.6%, and every macro input has moved the wrong way since.
  • The Fed leg is now broken. July CPI landed at 3.4% YoY Wednesday, then Thursday's PPI came in flat MoM / 4.7% YoY — below the 4.9% consensus and down from 5.5% — with a 3.1% drop in energy and a 5.7% slide in gasoline. CME FedWatch September hike odds fell from 55% a week ago to 42% post-CPI to 32% post-PPI. One caveat worth holding: core PPI excluding food, energy and trade services still ran +0.4%, which is the single thread the hawks have left going into the Aug 26 PCE.
  • The Hormuz prop is coming out. Brent fell 2.3% to $86.91 and WTI 2.6% to $81.09 as Iran and Oman confirmed an agreed framework to reopen the strait. Terms are not settled — Iran wants a 5–7% transit levy on cargo value against Oman's proposed ~3%, and Tehran is still tying full normalization to US concessions — but the direction of travel is toward reopening. Fewer euro per dollar when a corridor is signed: lower oil cuts the US inflation impulse and disproportionately helps the euro area, which imports its energy.
  • The euro side is unchanged and still working against you. July HICP 2.9% YoY, deposit rate 2.40%, and Q2 GDP confirmed at +0.4% QoQ — the strongest since early 2025. Euro-area one-year inflation swaps sit near 2.4%, above target, and markets price another 25bp ECB hike in September. June industrial production stalling is the only crack, and it is not enough to change the September call.

3. What's Driving It

  • Dollar — pinned at the 100 handle: DXY ~99.97, up 0.1% on the day after Thursday's close at 99.87, but down 1.6% from the late-July high of 101.60 and below both its 50- and 200-period moving averages. Resistance 100.30, support 99.50 (the Aug 7 two-month low). 🟡 Mixed — the level is holding, the momentum is not.
  • Fed — September hike odds down to 32%: Back-to-back cool prints (CPI 3.4%, core 2.5%; PPI flat MoM / 4.7% YoY) on top of the −23K July payrolls collapse have gutted the tightening case. Chair Kevin Warsh holds at 3.50–3.75% with three hawkish dissenters. Next decision Sept 15–16 with the dot plot. 🔴 Your largest active drag, and it deteriorated again this week.
  • Strait of Hormuz — framework agreed, terms unresolved: Iran and Oman have reached an agreed framework to reopen the strait; the sticking points are the transit levy (Iran 5–7% of cargo value vs. Oman's ~3%) and Tehran's demand that Washington end the war, withdraw troops and pay compensation before traffic normalizes. Brent $86.91 (−2.3%), WTI $81.09 (−2.6%). Transits remain a fraction of the ~130/day pre-conflict run rate. 🔴 Working against you — a signed corridor drops oil, drops the US inflation impulse, and drops your number.
  • ECB — hiking bias intact: Deposit rate 2.40%, unchanged in July after June's 25bp hike, the first in three years. July HICP 2.9%, one-year inflation swaps ~2.4%, Q2 GDP +0.4%. Next decision Sept 10, with markets pricing a 25bp increase. 🔴 A euro-supportive stance you are paying for.
  • Rate differential — level favors you, direction does not: Fed funds 3.75% vs ECB 2.40% — a 135bp gap still pulling capital toward the dollar. But the 10-year sits at 4.653% and falling on each soft print while Bunds hold at 3.133%, and the travel is Fed-dovish, ECB-hawkish. 🔴 Structural, and it will not resolve before the September meetings.

5. Two-Week Outlook

Base case: roughly €0.858 – €0.871 per dollar over the next two weeks. Today's €0.8669 sits at the top of that band — you are again being offered the good end of the expected range, but on a weaker footing than a week ago. The skew is negative: September hike odds have more than halved in seven sessions, oil is breaking down on a Hormuz framework, and the only two scheduled events that can push you higher (Aug 26 PCE, Jackson Hole) both land in the last third of the window.

More euro per dollar

€0.871 – €0.883

Core PCE runs hot on Aug 26, the Hormuz levy dispute stalls the corridor and Brent retakes $92, and Warsh pushes back hard at Jackson Hole against the 32% hike pricing. Needs at least two of the three — a narrower path than a week ago, but the +0.4% core-ex-trade-services PPI keeps it live.

Range-bound (most likely)

€0.858 – €0.871

Retail sales land near consensus, Hormuz stays framework-but-not-signed with Brent in the mid-$80s, and September hike odds drift in a 25–40% band while the market waits for PCE and the dot plot. Neither side of the differential resolves before September and you spend two weeks near where you are now.

Fewer euro per dollar

€0.845 – €0.858

The Oman corridor is signed and Brent drops into the low $70s, or retail sales miss and cool PCE flips the market from pricing no hike to pricing a cut. Either path alone is enough; both together take you through the April low of €0.8557.

This is not financial advice. FX markets are volatile and short-term moves are inherently unpredictable — treat the ranges above as scenario framing, not a guarantee. Cancel the €0.870 limit; the branch that fills it (a hot Aug 26 PCE or collapsed Hormuz talks) is now the minority case, and 0.3% of upside is not worth two weeks of that exposure.

Next cycle — set the rule now, not in the moment. For the next paycheck, stage it: 50% on receipt, 25% on any print of €0.875+, 25% by Sept 12 ahead of the Sept 15–16 FOMC regardless of level.
